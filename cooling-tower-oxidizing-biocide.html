Why cooling towers grow biology

A cooling tower is close to a purpose-built incubator. Water sits in the ideal temperature band, it is fully aerated, sunlight reaches the basin, and air drawn through the tower delivers a continuous supply of airborne organisms, dust and nutrients.

The visible result is algae — green growth on the basin walls and distribution deck. The costly result is invisible: biofilm forming inside heat exchanger tubes, where it insulates far more effectively than mineral scale. A biofilm layer a fraction of a millimetre thick can cut heat transfer more than a scale layer several times thicker.

Biofilm also shelters corrosion. Under-deposit corrosion develops beneath the film where inhibitors cannot reach, so a biologically fouled system corrodes faster even when the corrosion programme is otherwise correct.

How ZS-CT-302 works

Oxidising biocides kill on contact by attacking cell structure directly:

  • Fast contact kill — acts quickly on bacteria and algae throughout the circulating water.
  • Bromine component — stays effective at the higher pH values cooling water typically runs at, where chlorine alone weakens.
  • Residual protection — maintaining a measurable free residual keeps control continuous rather than intermittent.

Oxidising biocide handles routine day-to-day control. It is normally alternated with a non-oxidising biocide, because organisms sheltered inside established biofilm need a different mode of attack.

Common questions

What residual should I maintain?

1 to 2 ppm free residual in the circulating water. Test it regularly — a residual reading of zero means the biocide has been fully consumed and the system is unprotected between doses.

How often should I dose?

Most systems dose continuously or on a timed cycle to hold the residual. Systems with heavy fouling start with shock dosing to knock the population down, then move to maintenance dosing.

Should I use oxidising or non-oxidising biocide?

Both, alternating. Oxidising handles routine control; non-oxidising penetrates established biofilm that oxidising biocide cannot reach. Alternating also prevents organisms adapting to a single chemistry.

Will this remove algae already growing?

Shock dosing kills it, but dead algae needs physically removing from the basin — otherwise it decays and feeds the next growth cycle. Clean the basin after the shock dose.

Is it safe with my corrosion inhibitor?

They work as a programme, but dose them separately rather than mixing in one tank. Tell us your system and we will lay out the dosing points and sequence.

Does biocide help with Legionella risk?

Microbiological control is part of managing that risk, alongside system design, temperature control and regular cleaning. Tell us your installation type and we will advise on the programme.
