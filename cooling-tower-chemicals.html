Why cooling water attacks on three fronts

A cooling tower works by evaporating water. Salts do not evaporate — they stay behind and concentrate cycle after cycle, until calcium carbonate reaches saturation and deposits exactly where it hurts most: on the hot heat-exchanger surfaces where transfer efficiency matters.

At the same time the water is fully aerated by design, because it has just been sprayed through air. Oxygen-saturated water is corrosive to the mild steel and copper in the circuit, and pitting develops in the tubes while corrosion products circulate and settle as deposits of their own.

The third front is biological. Warm water, sunlight on the basin, and air drawn through the tower delivering dust and organisms make a cooling tower close to a purpose-built incubator. Algae blocks distribution nozzles; biofilm inside heat-exchanger tubes insulates far more effectively than mineral scale — a film a fraction of a millimetre thick can cut heat transfer more than scale several times thicker.

The three problems feed each other. Deposits create oxygen-concentration cells that accelerate corrosion underneath. Corrosion debris gives scale somewhere to anchor. Biofilm shelters both from the inhibitors meant to stop them. This is why cooling tower treatment is always a programme, never a single chemical.

A working chemical programme for a cooling tower

Runs continuously

Scale and corrosion inhibitor, dosed against circulating water volume and topped up as blowdown removes it.

Alternated

Oxidising biocide for routine control, non-oxidising as a periodic shock. Alternating stops organisms adapting to one chemistry and reaches biofilm that oxidising alone cannot.

Seasonal

Algaecide more often through summer, when sunlight and warmth accelerate growth. Clean the basin physically after treating.

Not a chemical, but essential

Cycle control. Blowdown sets how far the water concentrates. Chemical treatment widens the safe range — it does not remove the ceiling your make-up water sets.

Common questions

Which chemicals does a cooling tower need?

Scale and corrosion inhibitor running continuously, plus a biocide programme — oxidising for routine control, non-oxidising for periodic shock. Add algaecide for open basins exposed to sunlight.

What are cycles of concentration and why do they matter?

How many times the circulating water has concentrated relative to make-up — measure conductivity of both and divide. Higher cycles save water but push you closer to scaling. Your make-up water quality sets a practical limit; send us the analysis and we will tell you what it is.

Why do I need two different biocides?

Oxidising biocide is consumed at the surface of a biofilm and never reaches the organisms inside it. Non-oxidising chemistry penetrates the film. Alternating also prevents populations adapting to a single chemistry.

How much chemical will my system consume?

It is calculated on system holding volume — basin, piping and exchangers together — not tower tonnage alone. Send us your holding capacity and make-up water report and we will work out dose and monthly consumption.

How often should I test?

Conductivity and inhibitor residual weekly at minimum, along with pH and hardness. Biocide free residual should be checked more often — daily where possible.

Can I reduce blowdown to save water?

Only up to the limit your make-up water allows. Beyond that the circulating water concentrates past saturation and scales regardless of dose. Chemical treatment raises the ceiling; it does not remove it.
