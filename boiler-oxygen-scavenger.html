What dissolved oxygen does inside a boiler

Cold water holds a surprising amount of dissolved oxygen. Heat it inside a boiler and that oxygen comes out of solution and attacks the steel — not evenly across the surface, but at concentrated points. The result is pitting: deep, narrow holes that penetrate tube walls while the surrounding metal still looks sound.

This is what makes oxygen corrosion dangerous. General corrosion thins a tube gradually and gives you warning. Pitting drills through at a single point, and a tube can fail from a pit while a thickness survey elsewhere reads perfectly acceptable.

Oxygen enters through the make-up water, and through any air leaking into the condensate return system — which is why plants with long return lines and old fittings usually have a bigger oxygen problem than they expect.

How ZS-BO-201 works

The scavenger reacts chemically with dissolved oxygen so it can no longer attack metal:

  • Sulphite reaction — reacts with dissolved oxygen to form sulphate, removing it from the water.
  • Catalysed for speed — reacts fast enough at feed water temperature to work in the time available before the boiler.
  • Amine component — carries protection forward into the steam and condensate side, where oxygen also enters.

Dosing goes into the feed tank or feed line, ahead of the boiler. Where a deaerator is fitted, the scavenger handles the residual oxygen the deaerator cannot remove mechanically.

Common questions

How much oxygen scavenger should I dose?

Roughly three times the dissolved oxygen concentration in ppm, plus enough to hold a residual in the boiler water. Send us your feed water oxygen figure and boiler capacity and we will calculate the daily quantity.

Where should it be dosed?

Into the feed water tank or feed line before the boiler. Dosing into the feed tank gives more reaction time, which helps at lower temperatures.

Do I still need it if I have a deaerator?

Yes. A deaerator removes most oxygen mechanically but always leaves a residual, and that residual is enough to pit tubes over time. The scavenger handles what the deaerator leaves behind.

How do I know the dose is right?

Test for sulphite residual in the boiler water. A consistent measurable residual means you are covering the oxygen load; zero residual means you are dosing short.

What does oxygen pitting look like?

Deep, round, localised pits with the surrounding metal relatively unaffected, often under a small dark deposit. That pattern distinguishes it from general acid corrosion, which thins the surface evenly.

Can I use it with my sludge conditioner?

Yes, they do different jobs and are used together. Dose them separately rather than mixing in one tank — send us your boiler details and we will lay out the full programme.
