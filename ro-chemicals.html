What RO plant chemicals actually do

A reverse osmosis plant looks simple from outside — water in, clean water out. Inside the pressure vessel the picture is different. Around 60 to 75 percent of the feed passes through the membrane as permeate, and every dissolved salt that does not pass stays behind in a shrinking stream. By the last element, salts sit at three or four times their feed concentration.

That concentration is what drives every problem an RO plant develops. Calcium carbonate crosses its solubility limit and crystallises on the membrane. Silica polymerises. Bacteria settle in the warm wet channels and build a slime layer. Chlorine in municipal feed oxidises the polyamide layer permanently. Iron from borewell water deposits as oxide.

Chemicals are not an optional extra on an RO plant — they are what stands between your membranes and each of those failures. A plant running without antiscalant on hard feed water will scale within weeks. A plant on chlorinated municipal supply without SMBS will lose salt rejection permanently, and no cleaning brings it back.

A working chemical programme for an RO plant

Runs continuously

Antiscalant on every plant. SMBS wherever feed carries chlorine — municipal supply, or raw water storage that gets chlorinated.

Kept in stock for CIP

Both cleaners — acidic and alkaline. Most plants clean every 3–6 months; running out mid-fouling costs far more than the drum.

Used periodically

Biocide as a shock dose where bacterial counts climb, and as a preservation fill before any shutdown longer than a week.

Where needed

pH booster on plants where permeate runs through metal piping, or where product water must meet a pH specification.

Common questions

Which chemicals does a new RO plant need from day one?

Antiscalant from the first day of running, and SMBS if the feed carries any chlorine. Both cleaners should be in stock before you need them. Biocide comes in when the plant will sit idle or when counts start rising.

How much chemical will my plant consume per month?

It scales with feed flow. A 1,000 LPH plant at 3 ppm antiscalant uses roughly 2 kg a month running 10 hours a day. Send us your capacity, running hours and water report and we will work out your monthly consumption for each chemical.

Can I use one cleaner instead of two?

Not effectively. Acid removes minerals, alkali removes organics — they work on entirely different deposits and cannot be mixed. Plants that keep only one cleaner usually find their CIP results getting worse over time.

How do I know when to clean the membranes?

Three triggers, whichever comes first: normalised permeate flow down 10–15%, differential pressure up 15%, or salt passage up 10%. Keep a daily log — without a baseline you cannot tell what is normal for your plant.

Are these chemicals safe for drinking water plants?

Yes, at recommended dose levels. Antiscalant and SMBS leave with the reject or react away completely. Confirm you need food-grade material when ordering for packaged drinking water.
