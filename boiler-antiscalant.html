What scale costs a boiler

Scale is an insulator. A layer barely a millimetre thick across the heat-transfer surface forces the burner to work noticeably harder to deliver the same steam — that is fuel money burnt every hour the boiler runs.

The second cost is worse. Scale insulates the tube metal from the water that is supposed to cool it, so the metal runs hotter than design. Sustained overheating leads to bulging, cracking and tube failure — and boiler tube failures mean unplanned shutdown at best.

The hardness causing it is what gets past the softener. A softener at the end of its cycle, a missed regeneration, or a brine system running short all let hardness slip through in exactly the amounts that build scale.

How ZS-BO-202 works

The antiscalant intercepts hardness before it can form an adherent deposit:

  • Precipitates hardness — reacts with calcium and magnesium to form a soft particulate rather than hard crystalline scale.
  • Keeps particles dispersed — conditioning polymers stop particles agglomerating and bonding to the tube surface.
  • Blows down cleanly — the resulting sludge stays mobile and leaves with routine boiler blowdown.

This is a second line of defence, not a replacement for softening. Keeping feed hardness low is still the first job — the antiscalant handles the residue that gets through.

Where it is used

Steam boilers on softened feed

Standard protection where a softener supplies feed water — covering the hardness that slips through.

Plants with high make-up

Where a large fraction of feed is fresh make-up rather than returned condensate.

Older boilers

Units already carrying some deposit, where preventing further build-up is critical.

Textile and process plants

Continuous-running boilers where scale directly shows up as higher fuel consumption.

Common questions

How much boiler antiscalant should I dose?

5–15 ppm as product, set by your boiler pressure and feed hardness. Low pressure boilers sit at 5–10 ppm; medium and high pressure run 10–15 ppm. Send us your feed water report and boiler capacity and we will work out the daily quantity.

Do I still need a water softener?

Yes, absolutely. The antiscalant handles residual hardness — a few ppm. It cannot cope with raw hard feed water. Softening comes first; this protects against what gets through.

How often should I blow down?

Set the schedule by boiler water TDS against your boiler's recommended limit. Without blowdown the conditioned sludge stays inside and the treatment achieves nothing.

How do I know if my boiler already has scale?

Higher fuel consumption for the same steam output is the usual first sign. Confirm it during your next shutdown inspection — deposits on the water side of tubes are visible directly.

Can it remove scale that has already formed?

No. This is preventive. Existing hard scale needs mechanical or acid cleaning during a shutdown — tell us the boiler type and deposit and we will advise.

Can I dose it with the oxygen scavenger?

They run together as a programme but should be dosed separately rather than mixed in one tank. We can lay out the dosing points and rates for your boiler.
