What actually damages a boiler

A steam boiler fails in ways that give very little warning. The three main causes are all chemical, and all preventable — which is why a boiler running without a treatment programme is a boiler running on borrowed time.

Dissolved oxygen is the first. Cold make-up water carries oxygen; heating drives it out of solution and it attacks the steel at concentrated points. The result is pitting — deep narrow holes that punch through a tube wall while the metal around them still measures at full thickness. A tube can fail from a pit while your thickness survey reads perfectly acceptable.

Scale is the second, and it costs you money every hour before it costs you a tube. A layer barely a millimetre thick insulates the heat-transfer surface enough to raise fuel consumption measurably. Worse, it insulates the tube metal from the water meant to cool it, so the metal runs hotter than design — leading to bulging, cracking and failure.

Carbonic acid is the third, and it works on the part of the system people inspect least. Bicarbonate alkalinity in feed water breaks down at boiler temperature and releases CO₂, which travels with the steam and turns acidic when it condenses. Return lines corrode from the inside, and the iron they shed comes back to deposit inside the boiler.

A working chemical programme for a steam boiler

Runs continuously

Oxygen scavenger and antiscalant on every boiler. These two are not optional — they address the two failures that scrap boiler tubes.

Controlled by testing

Alkalinity builder dosed against your daily boiler water test. pH, alkalinity and TDS move together — read them as a set, never one alone.

Where condensate returns

Condensate inhibitor wherever you recover condensate. Test pH at the furthest return point, not the nearest — that is where systems fail.

Not a chemical, but essential

Blowdown. Every chemical here converts a problem into something that leaves with blowdown. Skip it and the treatment achieves nothing.

Common questions

Which chemicals does a steam boiler need?

At minimum, oxygen scavenger and antiscalant, dosed continuously. Add alkalinity builder for pH control, and condensate inhibitor wherever condensate is returned. Most industrial boilers run all four.

How much chemical will my boiler consume?

It depends on feed water quality, make-up ratio and boiler pressure. Send us your feed water report, boiler capacity and pressure rating and we will work out the daily dose and monthly consumption for each chemical.

Do I still need chemicals if I have a water softener?

Yes. A softener removes most hardness but never all of it, and it removes nothing at all when it is due for regeneration. It also does nothing about dissolved oxygen or condensate CO₂ — different problems entirely.

What tests should I run daily?

Boiler water pH, alkalinity and TDS as a minimum, plus sulphite residual to confirm oxygen scavenging. Condensate pH at least weekly. Without a log you are dosing blind.

How often should I blow down?

Set the schedule by boiler water TDS against your boiler's limit. Blowdown is what removes the conditioned sludge and controls concentration — dosing chemicals without adequate blowdown makes control impossible.

Can chemicals remove scale that has already formed?

No. These are preventive. Existing hard scale needs mechanical or acid cleaning during shutdown. Tell us the boiler type and deposit condition and we will advise on cleaning.
