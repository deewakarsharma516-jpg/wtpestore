Pumps for Water Treatment & Industry

A pump is the heart of almost every water and effluent system. The right choice depends on three things — the type of liquid (clean water, corrosive chemical, viscous fluid or dirty water), the flow rate you need (LPM or LPH) and the head/pressure the pump must overcome. Picking a pump on price alone usually leads to poor performance and early failure, so it is worth matching the pump to the duty.

High pressure vs centrifugal vs monoblock

For high-pressure jobs like RO membrane feed, car wash or misting, a multistage high-pressure pump is used because it builds strong pressure at a controlled flow. For moving large volumes at moderate pressure — plant circulation, transfer, cooling — a centrifugal pump is the standard, economical choice. For simple domestic and tank-filling duty, a compact monoblock pump (pump and motor on one shaft) is the easiest to install and maintain.

Chemical, rotary and sump pumps

When the liquid is corrosive — acids, alkalis, ETP/STP chemicals — a chemical process pump in PP, PVDF or SS316, often with a seal-less magnetic drive, prevents leaks and corrosion. For thick, viscous fluids a rotary (gear or lobe) pump gives smooth positive-displacement flow. To lift dirty water, drainage or sludge out of a pit, a submersible sump pump that can pass solids is the correct tool.

Dosing & metering pumps

Dosing pumps do a very different job: instead of moving bulk water, they inject a precise, adjustable dose of chemical into a line — antiscalant and antifoam for RO, chlorine for disinfection, pH correction and coagulant for ETP/STP. They come as electronic solenoid or motor-driven units with chemical-resistant heads, and correct sizing (ml per hour against line pressure) matters more than raw power.
