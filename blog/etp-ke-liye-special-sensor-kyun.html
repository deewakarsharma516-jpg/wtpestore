Why an ETP needs special sensors

Aqua Filtration System, Faridabad · 9+ years in water treatment · Updated September 2026

Short answer: ETP water contains oil, solids and constantly shifting pH, which blocks the reference junction of a standard sensor within two to three months. An ETP grade sensor has a special junction and a more robust body designed for those conditions.

The price difference is large — a standard pH sensor is ₹3,570 against ₹23,100 for the ETP pH sensor. But if a standard sensor has to be replaced four or five times a year, the expensive sensor is actually cheaper.

The real cost over two years

Using standard sensors

  • ₹3,570 × 4 replacements a year = ₹14,280 per year
  • Over 2 years = ₹28,560
  • Plus: plant interruptions each time, chemical wasted on wrong readings, and the labour

Using an ETP sensor

  • ₹23,100, lasting around 15 months
  • Over 2 years, about 1.6 sensors = ₹37,000
The conclusion: on sensor price alone the standard option looks cheaper. But once you add the repeated replacement effort, the chemical wasted on false readings, and the risk of failing discharge norms, the ETP sensor is the sensible choice — particularly where a pollution board is involved.

Where a standard sensor still works

Not every point in an ETP needs an expensive sensor:

LocationSensor
Raw effluent / collection tankETP sensor — the dirtiest point
Equalisation tankETP sensor
Aeration tankETP sensor preferable
After the clarifierStandard may be adequate
After filtration / final dischargeStandard is sufficient

So: use ETP sensors at the front of the plant and standard sensors at the end. That controls cost while keeping sensor life reasonable.

Extending sensor life

  1. Clean weekly — remove oil and film. Soak 15 minutes in mild detergent
  2. Position it well — where there is flow, not in a corner or stagnant area
  3. Fit a screen upstream — so coarse solids never reach the sensor
  4. Keep it away from the dosing point — avoiding sudden chemical shock
  5. Never let it dry out — store it in its cap with KCl solution when the plant is down
  6. Calibrate regularly — the full method is in our calibration guide

Keep a spare

In an ETP a failed sensor means more than a lost reading:

  • pH control fails and discharge norms may be breached, with a penalty
  • Dosing goes wrong — chemical wasted or treatment inadequate
  • A new sensor can take several days to arrive, and until then the plant runs manually

Keeping a spare sensor is therefore a sensible precaution, particularly on larger plants.

Frequently asked questions

Why does a standard pH sensor not last in an ETP?

ETP water contains oil, solids and sulphide which block the reference junction within two to three months. After that the sensor responds slowly and readings become unreliable.

Why is an ETP sensor so expensive?

It has a special double or open junction that resists blocking, and a body that withstands chemical attack. That is why it lasts twelve to eighteen months in an ETP where a standard sensor lasts only a few months.

Do I need expensive sensors everywhere in the ETP?

No. Use ETP sensors at the front of the plant, in the collection and equalisation tanks where the water is dirtiest. After filtration and at final discharge a standard sensor is usually adequate.

How can I extend the life of an ETP sensor?

Clean it weekly, position it where there is flow, fit a screen upstream so coarse solids do not reach it, and keep it away from the dosing point. Never let it dry out, and calibrate it regularly.

Should a spare sensor be kept for an ETP?

Yes, particularly on larger plants. If the sensor fails, pH control fails and discharge norms may be breached. A replacement can take several days to arrive, so a spare avoids that risk.
