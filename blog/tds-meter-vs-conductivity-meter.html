TDS meter vs conductivity meter — what is the difference

Aqua Filtration System, Faridabad · 9+ years in water treatment · Updated September 2026

Short answer: Both measure the same thing — dissolved salts in water. A conductivity meter measures electrical conductance directly, in µS/cm, and a TDS meter multiplies that same reading by a factor to display it in ppm. So a TDS meter is a conductivity meter with a calculation added.

That is why both cost roughly the same — the TDS Meter TI 350 is ₹2,730 and so is the Conductivity Meter CI 350.

Understanding the difference

Pure water does not conduct electricity. Once salts dissolve in it, it begins to conduct — the more salts, the higher the conductivity.

ConductivityTDS
What it measuresAbility to conduct electricityTotal dissolved solids
UnitµS/cm (microsiemens)ppm or mg/L
How it is obtainedMeasured directlyConductivity × factor
Where it is usedIndustry, DM plant, boilerDrinking water, RO
Conversion: TDS (ppm) = Conductivity (µS/cm) × 0.5 to 0.7
A factor of 0.65 is commonly used, so 1,000 µS/cm is roughly 650 ppm TDS.

Why the factor varies from 0.5 to 0.7

Because different salts conduct differently. Sodium chloride conducts more, calcium carbonate less. So the factor shifts depending on which salts dominate in your water.

What this means in practice: the number on a TDS meter is an approximation, not an exact figure. A laboratory report may show a different TDS — that is normal and does not mean the meter is faulty.

Meter or controller — an important distinction

  • Meter (TI 350, CI 350): shows a reading only. You have to look at it.
  • Controller (CT 650): reads and also operates a relay — for example stopping the plant or diverting water when TDS rises.

If you want poor quality water kept out of the tank automatically, you need a controller, not a meter. There is also the ROMATIC controller, which manages the whole RO plant.

Calculating rejection

TDS tells you how well the RO plant is performing:

Rejection % = (Feed TDS − Permeate TDS) ÷ Feed TDS × 100

Example: feed 1,000 ppm, permeate 40 ppm → (1,000 − 40) ÷ 1,000 × 100 = 96 percent

A new membrane should give 96 to 98 percent. If it falls below 90 percent the membrane is nearing end of life — see our RO monitoring guide.

Two common mistakes

  1. Fitting a meter only on permeate — without feed TDS you cannot calculate rejection at all. Both are needed.
  2. Leaving the sensor in air — the sensor should stay immersed. Drying out shortens its life.

Frequently asked questions

What is the difference between TDS and conductivity?

They measure the same property. Conductivity is measured directly as the ability of water to conduct electricity, while TDS is that same reading multiplied by a factor and displayed in ppm. So a TDS meter is essentially a conductivity meter with a calculation added.

How do I convert conductivity to TDS?

Multiply the conductivity by a factor of about zero point five to zero point seven. A factor of zero point six five is commonly used, so one thousand microsiemens is roughly six hundred and fifty ppm.

Should I use a TDS or a conductivity meter on an RO plant?

For drinking water plants a TDS meter is better because people understand ppm. For a DM plant, boiler or cooling tower a conductivity meter is preferable, because the values there are either very low or very high.

What is the difference between a meter and a controller?

A meter only displays a reading, which somebody has to look at. A controller has a relay that can stop the plant or divert water when the reading goes out of range, so poor quality water never reaches the tank.

When is temperature compensation necessary?

When the water temperature is well away from twenty five degrees, as in a boiler or cooling tower. Conductivity changes with temperature, so without compensation the reading will be misleading.
