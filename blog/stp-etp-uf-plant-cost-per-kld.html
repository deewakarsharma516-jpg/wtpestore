What decides the cost per KLD

  • Type of wastewater: domestic sewage is simpler than industrial effluent, which may need chemical treatment
  • Technology: MBBR, SBR or conventional — see the comparison
  • Civil or packaged: RCC tanks vs a skid / container plant
  • Treated water use: discharge only, or reuse for flushing, gardening or cooling — reuse may need tertiary treatment (filters, UF)
  • Automation: panels with automatic blower and pump control, and remote monitoring

Sizing correctly

  • STP: sewage is about 80 percent of the water used. A building using 25,000 L/day produces ~20 KLD of sewage.
  • ETP: measure the actual effluent from each process, and add a margin for peak days.
  • UF: size on the treated water you want to reuse, after the STP or ETP.
Do not undersize. A plant running above its capacity does not meet the discharge norms, and enlarging it later costs more than building the right size now.

Where UF fits

UF after an STP or ETP removes suspended solids and bacteria, giving clear water for reuse, or a good feed for an RO if you want to recover water for process use. See UF vs RO.

Frequently asked questions

What is the cost of an STP per KLD?

Our current prices from 10 to 50 KLD are shown in the live table on this page. Cost per KLD falls as plant size increases.

What does KLD mean?

Kilo litres per day. 1 KLD equals 1,000 litres of wastewater treated per day.

How do I calculate the STP capacity for a building?

Sewage is about 80 percent of water used, so a building using 25,000 litres a day needs roughly a 20 KLD STP.

Why is an ETP more expensive than an STP?

Industrial effluent often needs chemical treatment, pH correction and sludge handling in addition to biological treatment.

Is UF needed after an STP?

Only if the treated water is reused. UF removes suspended solids and bacteria and gives clear water for reuse or RO feed.
