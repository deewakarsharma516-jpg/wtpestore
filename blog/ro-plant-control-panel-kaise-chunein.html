How to select a control panel for an RO plant

Aqua Filtration System, Faridabad · 9+ years in water treatment · Updated September 2026

Short answer: You need three details to select a panel — how many pumps it must run, the HP of each, and the phase (single or three). The Astero model number tells you this directly: 11NXT means both pumps single phase, 13NXT means raw water single and HP pump three phase, and 33NXT means both three phase.

Once you understand that, selection becomes straightforward. Below is the full chart and the mistakes we see most often.

How to read the model number

The digits in the name indicate the phase of each pump:

NameMeaning
11NXTRaw water pump single phase, HP pump single phase
13NXTRaw water pump single phase, HP pump three phase
33NXTRaw water pump three phase, HP pump three phase
333NXTThree pumps — RWP, HPP and a transfer or circulation pump, all three phase
What RWP and HPP mean: RWP is the Raw Water Pump, which draws water from the tank. HPP is the High Pressure Pump, which builds pressure across the membrane. Every RO plant has at least these two.

What the panel actually does

  • Dry run protection — stops the pump when the tank empties. Full details in our dry run guide
  • Automatic sequencing — the RWP starts first and the HPP a few seconds later, so the pumps are not shocked
  • Stops on tank full — via a level switch
  • High and low pressure protection — via pressure switches
  • TDS or conductivity display — on some models, with a sensor

What the WTP version is

Some models carry the WTP designation, such as the ASTERO 33NXT WTP (₹12,600). This is a configuration for a complete water treatment plant, which also handles filter and softener sequencing rather than RO alone.

Four common mistakes

  1. Ordering without confirming the phase — an 11NXT supplied in place of a 13NXT will not run the HP pump at all
  2. No margin on HP — a panel rated exactly for a 5HP pump is fine today, but if the pump is ever upsized the panel has to change too
  3. Ignoring the cut-out size — if it must fit an existing board, the size matters. 190Hx222V and 305Hx225V are different
  4. Buying only the panel and no protection devices — a panel cannot prevent dry running by itself; a pressure switch or level switch has to be fitted

What to tell us when ordering

  1. How many pumps — two or three
  2. The HP and phase of each pump
  3. Any limit on the cut-out size
  4. Whether remote monitoring or RS485 is required

Frequently asked questions

What is the difference between an Astero 11NXT and a 33NXT?

The digits indicate the phase of each pump. On the 11NXT both pumps are single phase, while on the 33NXT both are three phase. In between there is the 13NXT, where the raw water pump is single phase and the high pressure pump is three phase.

Which panel does my RO plant need?

It depends on the pumps rather than the plant capacity. Check the HP and phase of your raw water pump and high pressure pump, then select accordingly. Smaller plants usually take an 11NXT and larger ones a 33NXT.

Is the SA series or the NXT series better?

The NXT is newer, with a better display and an RS485 option. The price difference is small, so if there is any chance of automation or remote monitoring later, the NXT is the better buy.

Will the panel prevent dry running on its own?

The panel includes the protection function, but it needs a pressure switch or level switch connected to work. Buying the panel alone will not achieve it, so order the switches at the same time.

Why does the cut-out size matter?

If the panel has to fit into an existing board, its cut-out size must match. Astero models are commonly 190 by 222 or 305 by 225, which are quite different. Measure your board before ordering.
