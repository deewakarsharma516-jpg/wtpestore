Multigrade filter (MGF) — what it does, sizes, flow and price

Aqua Filtration System, Faridabad · 9+ years in water treatment · Updated October 2026

Short answer: A multigrade filter (MGF) is a pressure sand filter with several layers of graded media — pebbles, gravel and fine silica sand — inside an FRP vessel. It removes suspended solids, silt, sand and turbidity down to about 20–25 micron. It is the first stage before a softener, carbon filter or RO plant, and it is cleaned by a simple daily backwash.

What a multigrade filter removes — and what it does not

RemovesDoes NOT remove
Sand, silt, mud and rust particlesDissolved salts (TDS)
Turbidity (cloudy water)Hardness (calcium, magnesium)
Suspended solids above ~25 micronChlorine, smell and colour
Oxidised iron (after aeration / chlorination)Bacteria and viruses

So an MGF is a protection stage. It keeps the cartridge filter, softener resin and RO membranes from choking. For smell and chlorine you add an activated carbon filter; for hardness, a water softener.

How the media layers work

Water enters at the top and flows down. Coarse media at the bottom supports the finer media above it:

  • Top — fine silica sand: traps the smallest particles
  • Middle — coarse sand and gravel: holds larger dirt and spreads the flow
  • Bottom — pebbles: supports the bed and protects the bottom strainers

Because dirt is caught through the depth of the bed and not just on the surface, an MGF runs longer between backwashes than a single-layer sand filter.

Backwash — the one job that keeps it working

Dirt collects in the bed. If it is not washed out, pressure drop rises and dirt starts passing through. With the multiport valve:

  1. Backwash 5–10 minutes (10–15 minutes on the largest vessel) — water flows upward and lifts the dirt out to drain
  2. Rinse 2–3 minutes — settles the bed back in place
  3. Service — back to normal filtering

Do it daily, or as soon as the inlet and outlet pressure gauges show a difference of about 0.5–0.7 kg/cm². If backwash does not clear it, see our MPV backwash troubleshooting guide.

When the media needs replacing

  • Output is still cloudy even after a full backwash
  • Media is visible at the drain during backwash (media loss)
  • The bed has become hard or forms channels

Under normal use, sand and gravel last 2–3 years; very dirty feed water shortens that.

Frequently asked questions

What does a multigrade filter remove?

Sand, silt, mud, rust particles and turbidity — suspended solids above roughly 20 to 25 micron. It does not remove TDS, hardness, chlorine or bacteria.

How often should a multigrade filter be backwashed?

Daily for most borewell water, for 5 to 10 minutes followed by a 2 to 3 minute rinse. Backwash sooner if the pressure difference across the vessel goes above about 0.5 to 0.7 kg/cm².

Which multigrade filter size do I need?

Choose on peak flow. Up to 750 LPH an 844 vessel is enough, up to 1500 LPH a 1054, up to 2500 LPH a 1354 and up to 3500 LPH a 1465.

Is a multigrade filter the same as a sand filter?

It is a pressure sand filter with several graded layers instead of one. The layered bed holds more dirt, so it runs longer between backwashes.

Do I still need a cartridge filter after an MGF?

Yes, before an RO plant. The MGF removes most solids, and a 5 micron cartridge filter is the final guard in front of the high pressure pump.
