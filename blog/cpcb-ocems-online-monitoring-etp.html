CPCB online effluent monitoring (OCEMS) — what ETPs need to know

Aqua Filtration System, Faridabad · 9+ years in water treatment · Updated October 2026

Short answer: OCEMS (Online Continuous Effluent Monitoring System) measures treated effluent quality — typically pH, BOD, COD, TSS and sometimes flow and other parameters — around the clock and sends the data automatically to the CPCB / State Pollution Control Board server. Many industries are required to install it as a condition of their consent to operate.

Who needs it

CPCB has directed industries in the highly polluting categories, and units with large discharge, to install online monitoring at the ETP outlet. Your consent to operate (CTO) from the State Board states whether it applies to you and which parameters are required. If your CTO mentions online monitoring, the board can act on non-compliance.

Check first: read the conditions in your CTO, or ask your environmental consultant, before buying — the required parameter list decides the system.

Parameters usually monitored

ParameterWhat it shows
pHAcidity / alkalinity of the discharge
BODBiodegradable organic load
CODTotal organic and chemical load
TSSSuspended solids
Turbidity, TDSClarity and dissolved salts
FlowQuantity of discharge

How the system works

  1. A multi-parameter probe (a UV-spectral sensor for BOD, COD, TSS and turbidity, plus pH and TDS sensors) sits in the treated effluent channel or a sample tank
  2. A controller shows the readings locally and logs them
  3. A modem sends the data to the board server at fixed intervals
  4. Alerts warn the operator when a value crosses its limit

Keeping it compliant

  • Clean the sensors on schedule — a fouled probe gives false high readings
  • Calibrate as per the maker's schedule (see our calibration guide)
  • Renew the data hosting every year so the connection does not lapse
  • Investigate every exceedance alert — the board sees the same data

Frequently asked questions

What is OCEMS?

Online Continuous Effluent Monitoring System — equipment that measures treated effluent parameters continuously and sends the data to the CPCB or State Pollution Control Board server.

Which parameters does an ETP online monitoring system measure?

Usually pH, BOD, COD and TSS, often with turbidity, TDS and flow. The exact list is set in your consent to operate.

Is online effluent monitoring compulsory?

For many industries in highly polluting categories and units with large discharge, yes. Your consent to operate states whether it applies.

How does the data reach the pollution board?

A modem in the system sends the logged readings to the board server at fixed intervals; data hosting is renewed yearly.

How often should the sensors be cleaned?

As per the maker's schedule, usually weekly checks with periodic calibration. Fouled sensors give false readings.
