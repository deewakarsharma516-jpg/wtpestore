Water treatment plant for hotels and hospitals — what you need

Aqua Filtration System, Faridabad · 9+ years in water treatment · Updated October 2026

Short answer: A hotel or hospital usually needs four systems: a filter + softener for all general water (to protect geysers, boilers and plumbing), an RO + UV plant for drinking and kitchen water, a boiler / laundry feed softener, and an STP to treat and reuse sewage. Each is sized separately.

The four systems

SystemTreatsWhy
Filter + softenerAll incoming waterStops scale in geysers, pipes, kitchen equipment and laundry
RO + UVDrinking and cooking waterLow TDS, bacteria-free water for guests and patients
Boiler / laundry softenerBoiler feed, laundryPrevents boiler scale and saves detergent
STPSewageDischarge compliance and reuse for flushing and gardening

Rough water demand for planning

  • Hotel: 300–500 litres per room per day (higher for luxury and banquets)
  • Hospital: 400–450 litres per bed per day
  • Drinking + kitchen is only a small share — usually 5–10 percent of total
  • Sewage is roughly 80 percent of the water used
Example: a 40-room hotel at 400 L/room = 16,000 L/day. Drinking and kitchen ~1,200 L/day → a 250–500 LPH RO. Sewage ~13 KLD → a 15–20 KLD STP.

Points specific to hospitals

  • Dialysis and laboratories need separate high-purity water — usually a dedicated RO with polishing, not the drinking water line
  • Hospital effluent from labs may need an ETP in addition to the STP
  • UV on stored water lines, and regular tank cleaning, are essential

Frequently asked questions

What water treatment does a hotel need?

Typically a filter and softener for general water, an RO with UV for drinking and kitchen water, a softener for the boiler and laundry, and an STP for sewage.

How much water does a hotel use per room?

About 300 to 500 litres per room per day for planning, higher for luxury properties and banquets.

What size RO does a hotel need?

RO is only for drinking and kitchen water, about 5 to 10 percent of total use. A 40-room hotel usually needs a 250 to 500 LPH RO.

Does a hospital need an STP?

Yes, and hospitals with laboratories may also need an ETP for lab effluent.

Can treated STP water be reused?

Yes, for toilet flushing, gardening and cooling, after proper treatment and disinfection.
