What changes the price

  • Raw water TDS: high TDS (above ~2,000 ppm) needs more membranes, a stronger pump and lower recovery
  • Hardness and iron: may need a softener or iron removal before the RO
  • Pre-treatment: multigrade filter, carbon filter, softener, dosing pumps
  • Membrane brand and size: 4040 or 8040 — see 4040 vs 8040
  • Frame and vessels: MS powder coated, SS, FRP or SS membrane housings
  • Automation: manual, semi-automatic, or fully automatic with TDS control and remote monitoring

What a complete RO plant includes

  1. Raw water pump
  2. Multigrade filter and carbon filter
  3. Antiscalant dosing pump
  4. 5 micron cartridge filter
  5. High pressure pump
  6. RO membranes in pressure housings
  7. Control panel, flow meters, pressure gauges and TDS meter

Running costs to budget for

  • Electricity for the raw water and high pressure pumps
  • Antiscalant — see dosing calculation
  • Cartridges every 1–2 months
  • CIP chemicals 2–4 times a year
  • Membranes every 2–4 years depending on feed water

Choosing the capacity

Divide the litres you need per day by the hours you can run the plant. 20,000 litres a day over 10 hours = 2,000 LPH. Then allow a margin for future demand. Our sizing guide covers recovery and reject water.

Frequently asked questions

What is the price of an industrial RO plant?

Our current prices from 200 LPH to 5,000 LPH are shown in the live table on this page, exclusive of GST. The final price depends on raw water quality, pre-treatment and automation.

Why do RO plant prices vary so much?

Raw water TDS, hardness, the pre-treatment needed, membrane type, frame material and automation level all change the cost.

What is included in an RO plant?

Raw water pump, multigrade and carbon filters, antiscalant dosing, cartridge filter, high pressure pump, membranes in housings, and the control panel with instruments.

What are the running costs of an RO plant?

Electricity, antiscalant, cartridges, periodic CIP chemicals, and membrane replacement every 2 to 4 years.

How do I choose the RO capacity?

Divide the litres needed per day by the running hours, for example 20,000 litres over 10 hours is 2,000 LPH, then add a margin.
