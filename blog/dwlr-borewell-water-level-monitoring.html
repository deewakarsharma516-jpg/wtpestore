DWLR — digital water level recorder for borewells, explained

Aqua Filtration System, Faridabad · 9+ years in water treatment · Updated October 2026

Short answer: A DWLR (Digital Water Level Recorder) is a pressure-type level transmitter lowered into a borewell or piezometer. It records the groundwater level automatically, and with a modem and SIM it sends the data to a server — so readings are available without anyone visiting the well. Groundwater NOC conditions often require exactly this.

Why industries are asked to install it

Units drawing groundwater under an NOC from the groundwater authority are commonly required to install a piezometer with a DWLR and telemetry, and a flow meter on the borewell outlet. The exact conditions are written in your NOC — check it to see what is required at your site.

How it works

  1. A submersible level transmitter hangs on a vented cable below the lowest expected water level
  2. It measures the pressure of the water column above it and converts it to depth
  3. The indicator at the top shows and logs the level
  4. The modem sends readings to the server, viewable on a web portal

Choosing the cable length

The transmitter must sit below the lowest water level the well will reach in summer, with a margin. As a guide:

Do not guess: measure the summer water level, or ask the borewell driller. A transmitter left hanging above the water reads zero.

Installation tips

  • Install in a dedicated piezometer or a well that is not pumping, or the reading jumps with every pump start
  • Keep the vent tube of the cable open and dry at the top
  • Lock the cover — the well head should be tamper-proof
  • Renew the SIM and data hosting every year

Frequently asked questions

What is a DWLR?

A Digital Water Level Recorder — a pressure-type level transmitter placed in a borewell or piezometer that records the groundwater level automatically.

Is a DWLR compulsory for borewells?

Many groundwater NOC conditions for industries require a piezometer with a DWLR and telemetry. Check the conditions in your NOC.

Which cable length do I need?

Long enough for the transmitter to sit below the lowest summer water level with margin — a 50 m cable for levels up to about 35 m, 100 m for deeper levels.

Can the DWLR data be seen online?

Yes, with the modem and SIM the readings are sent to a server and viewed on a web portal.

Can the DWLR be installed in the pumping borewell?

It is better in a separate piezometer, because pumping makes the level reading jump.
