pH correction dosing — designing an acid or alkali system

Aqua Filtration System, Faridabad · 9+ years in water treatment · Updated September 2026

Short answer: A pH correction system needs three things — a dosing pump (PVDF for acid), a pH sensor, and a controller that runs the pump from the sensor reading. Alternatively, everything in one unit such as the Edose NEUTRA with pH/ORP sensor (₹25,725).

Manual dosing does not work here, because the pH of the water changes constantly — particularly in an ETP.

Where pH correction is needed

Why RO permeate pH is raised: RO water is aggressive — with the minerals removed it dissolves metal from pipes, tanks and fittings. A pH booster brings the pH above 7. More detail on the RO chemicals page.

Three ways to build it

1. Simplest — manual dosing

A standard dosing pump at a fixed setting. This works only where the feed water pH is fairly steady.

Special considerations for ETP

ETP water is the most difficult — it carries solids, oil and a constantly shifting pH. So:

  • A standard pH sensor fails quickly — an ETP grade sensor is needed (₹23,100)
  • Dosing in both directions is required, acid and alkali, because pH swings either way
  • Mixing is essential — the water must be properly mixed after chemical injection, or the sensor reads incorrectly

Five points when installing

  1. Keep the sensor away from the injection point — at least 10 to 15 seconds of flow travel, so that what is measured is fully mixed water
  2. PVDF throughout for acid — pump, tubing and level switch. See the material guide
  3. Calibrate the sensor monthly with buffer solutions. Without calibration the controller doses on a wrong reading
  4. Set a dead band such as 6.8 to 7.2, otherwise the pump switches on and off continuously
  5. Fit an alarm for pH outside range. A relay output is useful here

Safety — please take this seriously

Acid dosing is hazardous. Always:
  • Wear gloves, eye protection and an apron — every time
  • Add acid to water, never water to acid
  • Keep a water tap nearby for eye washing
  • Store acid and alkali tanks well apart — mixed together they react violently
  • Label every tank clearly with its contents

Frequently asked questions

Which chemicals are used for pH correction?

Sulphuric or hydrochloric acid to lower pH, and caustic soda, lime or a pH booster to raise it. On RO permeate a pH booster is normally used because only a small increase is needed.

Can manual dosing control pH?

Only where the incoming water pH is fairly steady. In an ETP the pH changes constantly, so an automatic system with a sensor is the only thing that works; otherwise the dose is alternately too much and too little.

How often should a pH sensor be calibrated?

At least once a month using buffer solutions. Without calibration the controller works from a wrong reading and doses incorrectly. In difficult water such as ETP, calibration may be needed more often.

What safety precautions are needed for acid dosing?

Always wear gloves, eye protection and an apron. Add acid to water and never water to acid. Keep a water tap nearby, and store acid and alkali tanks well apart because they react violently if mixed.

Why will a standard pH sensor not work in an ETP?

ETP water contains oil and solids that quickly foul and block a standard sensor junction. A purpose-built ETP sensor is made to withstand those conditions, which is why it costs considerably more.
