RO membrane housings — pressure rating and selection

Aqua Filtration System, Faridabad · 9+ years in water treatment · Updated September 2026

Short answer: The housing must match three things — the element size, your operating pressure with a safety margin, and your piping arrangement. Getting the pressure rating wrong is the dangerous mistake, because a housing failure under pressure is a serious safety incident.

Pressure rating — the critical specification

Housings are rated for maximum working pressure. Your operating pressure must be comfortably below that rating, not at it.

ApplicationOperating pressureHousing rating needed
Low pressure RO7 – 10 kg/cm²At least 15 kg/cm²
Brackish water RO10 – 15 kg/cm²At least 21 kg/cm²
High recovery brackish15 – 20 kg/cm²At least 28 kg/cm²
Seawater RO50 – 70 kg/cm²Special high pressure vessels
Never operate a housing near its rated pressure. Pressure surges from valve operation or pump starting can momentarily exceed the steady reading. Always allow margin, and fit a high pressure switch (₹546) so the pump stops before pressure becomes dangerous.

Connection sizes

Housings are available with different port sizes to suit your piping. We stock membrane housings in 25NB, 40NB and 50NB.

Match the port to your line size — see our NB size guide for the relationship between flow and pipe size.

Elements per housing

Housings hold one or more elements in series:

ElementsSuitsNote
1 elementSmall plantsSimplest, easiest to service
2 elementsMedium plantsCommon arrangement
3 elementsLarger plantsBetter recovery per housing
4 – 6 elementsIndustrialLong vessel, needs space to load

Allow space at one end to withdraw the elements. A six element vessel needs roughly its own length again in clear space, which people often forget when planning the layout.

Material

  • FRP: the standard choice — light, corrosion free, and available in all pressure ratings
  • SS: used where mechanical protection matters or in specific industrial settings, but heavier and more expensive

For most water treatment work FRP is correct — the same reasoning as in our FRP vs MS guide.

Port arrangement

TypeDescriptionSuits
Side portFeed and reject on the sideMost common, easier piping
End portFeed and reject at the endsCompact skids

The permeate port is normally at one or both ends regardless.

O-rings and brine seals

These small parts matter more than people expect:

Element O-rings

Seal the permeate tube connection. A failed O-ring lets feed water leak directly into the permeate — so permeate TDS rises sharply while the membrane itself is perfectly fine.

Brine seal

A lip seal around the element that forces all feed water to pass through the element rather than around it. A worn brine seal means water bypasses the membrane, so flow looks high but rejection is poor.

Always replace O-rings and the brine seal when changing elements. They cost very little, and a worn seal produces exactly the symptoms that make people wrongly condemn a new membrane.

Fitting an element correctly

  1. Clean the housing internally and check for scoring
  2. Fit a new brine seal, with the lip facing the feed end — reversed, it does nothing
  3. Lubricate O-rings with glycerine or approved lubricant, never oil
  4. Slide the element in from the feed end, gently
  5. Fit the end caps and retaining rings securely
  6. Check the retaining ring is properly seated — this is what holds the pressure
  7. Pressurise slowly and check for leaks
The retaining ring is a safety component. If it is not fully seated, the end cap can be forced out under pressure. Always confirm it is correctly located before pressurising.

Common housing problems

SymptomLikely cause
Permeate TDS suddenly highO-ring failed, feed leaking into permeate
High flow but poor rejectionBrine seal worn, water bypassing
Leak at the end capCap O-ring, or retaining ring not seated
Element hard to removeScaling, or brine seal stuck
Housing bulgingOver-pressure — replace immediately

Safety when servicing

  • Depressurise completely before opening any end cap
  • Confirm the pressure gauge reads zero
  • Open the permeate line to release trapped pressure
  • Stand to one side when removing the retaining ring
  • Never attempt to open a housing while the pump is running

What to specify when ordering

  1. Element size — 4040, 8040 and so on
  2. Number of elements per housing
  3. Operating pressure
  4. Port size in NB and side or end port
  5. Available space for element removal

Frequently asked questions

What pressure rating should a membrane housing have?

Comfortably above your operating pressure, not at it. For brackish water RO running at ten to fifteen kg per cm2, a housing rated at least twenty one kg is appropriate, because pressure surges can momentarily exceed the steady reading.

What does the brine seal do?

It is a lip seal around the element that forces all feed water to pass through the membrane rather than around it. A worn brine seal lets water bypass, so flow looks high but rejection is poor.

Why did permeate TDS rise suddenly after changing a membrane?

Most often a failed or badly fitted O-ring on the permeate tube connection, which lets feed water leak directly into the permeate. The membrane itself may be perfectly fine, so check the seals before condemning it.

Which way round does the brine seal go?

The lip must face the feed end so that pressure pushes it outward against the housing wall. Fitted the wrong way round it does nothing at all and water simply bypasses the element.

How much space is needed to service a housing?

Roughly the length of the vessel again in clear space at one end, so that elements can be withdrawn. This is easy to overlook when planning a layout and awkward to fix afterwards.
