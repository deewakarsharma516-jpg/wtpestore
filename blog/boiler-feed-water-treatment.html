Boiler feed water treatment — softener, RO or DM plant?

Aqua Filtration System, Faridabad · 9+ years in water treatment · Updated October 2026

Short answer: Almost every boiler needs zero-hardness feed water, so a softener is the minimum. Low-pressure boilers usually run fine on softened water plus chemical dosing. Where the raw water TDS is high, add RO to reduce blowdown. High-pressure boilers need DM (demineralised) water. Chemical dosing — oxygen scavenger and alkalinity builder — is needed in all cases.

Why feed water treatment matters

Hardness forms scale on the tubes. Even a thin layer cuts heat transfer, raises fuel use and can overheat and damage tubes. Dissolved oxygen causes pitting corrosion, and high TDS forces more blowdown, wasting heat and water. See boiler scale prevention.

Which treatment for which boiler

SituationFeed water treatment
Low-pressure boiler, raw water TDS moderateFilter + softener + chemical dosing
Low / medium pressure, raw water TDS highSoftener + RO (less blowdown, less fuel waste)
High-pressure boilerDM plant or RO + mixed bed polisher
Thermic fluid heaterNot water — no feed water treatment
Always follow the boiler maker's feed water limits — they are given in the boiler manual and decide which treatment you need.

Chemical dosing — needed in every case

  • Oxygen scavenger — removes dissolved oxygen that causes pitting
  • Alkalinity / pH builder — keeps boiler water in the safe pH range
  • Boiler antiscalant — keeps any trace hardness from sticking as scale
  • Condensate corrosion inhibitor — protects return lines

Sizing the softener for a boiler

Feed water per hour is roughly equal to the boiler's steam output plus blowdown — a 1 TPH boiler needs about 1,000–1,100 litres per hour of feed. Use our softener sizing guide with your hardness to find the resin volume, and keep a standby or a duplex softener so the boiler never runs on hard water during regeneration.

Frequently asked questions

Does a boiler need a water softener?

Yes. Almost every boiler needs zero-hardness feed water to prevent scale, so a softener is the minimum treatment.

When is an RO plant needed for boiler feed?

When raw water TDS is high. RO reduces TDS so less blowdown is needed, saving fuel and water.

When is a DM plant needed for a boiler?

For high-pressure boilers, which need demineralised water as per the maker's feed water limits.

Which chemicals are dosed in boiler water?

An oxygen scavenger, an alkalinity or pH builder, a boiler antiscalant, and a condensate corrosion inhibitor for return lines.

How much feed water does a 1 TPH boiler need?

About 1,000 to 1,100 litres per hour, covering steam output plus blowdown.
