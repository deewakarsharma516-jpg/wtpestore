UF plant backwash — cycle timing and operation

Aqua Filtration System, Faridabad · 9+ years in water treatment · Updated September 2026

Short answer: A UF membrane fouls at its surface within minutes rather than months, so it is backwashed automatically every 20 to 60 minutes. Each cycle takes only a minute or two. Because this happens continuously, a UF plant cannot run without a controller.

Controller options include the ASTERO UF NG (₹15,750), UF NXT I (₹23,100) and UFMATIC (₹17,850).

Why UF needs frequent backwashing

UF is a surface filtration process. Particles are retained on the membrane surface rather than passing into it, so a cake layer builds up quickly and flow falls.

The advantage of hollow fibre construction is that flow can be reversed to lift that cake off, which is not possible with a spiral wound RO element.

The backwash cycle

  1. Service — normal filtration, 20 to 60 minutes
  2. Backwash — permeate is forced back through the membrane, lifting the cake, 30 to 60 seconds
  3. Forward flush — feed water sweeps the loosened material out, 20 to 40 seconds
  4. Drain — waste goes out
  5. Return to service

The whole cycle takes one to two minutes, so plant availability remains high.

Setting the interval

Feed waterTypical interval
Clean, low turbidity45 – 60 minutes
Moderate turbidity30 – 45 minutes
River or high turbidity20 – 30 minutes
Monsoon conditionsShorten as required
Set it on transmembrane pressure, not just time. If pressure rises faster than expected, shorten the interval. If it stays flat, you can lengthen it and save water and downtime.

CEB — chemically enhanced backwash

Ordinary backwash removes loose material, but organic and biological fouling gradually accumulates. A CEB adds chemical to the backwash water:

  • Alkaline or hypochlorite CEB — for organic and biological fouling
  • Acid CEB — for mineral and iron deposits

Typically done daily or every few days, depending on the feed water. It is far shorter than a full CIP and keeps the membrane performing.

CIP — full cleaning

When CEB no longer restores performance, a full CIP is needed — soaking the membranes in cleaning solution for a longer period. This is done monthly to quarterly depending on conditions.

The principles are the same as for RO — see our CIP guide — but check the UF membrane's own chemical and pH limits, which differ from RO.

Water consumption

Backwash uses permeate water, so it reduces net recovery. Even so, UF typically recovers 90 to 95 percent — considerably better than RO.

Backwash water can often be returned to the raw water tank for re-treatment, which improves recovery further, provided the solids are managed.

Where UF fits

UF removes turbidity, bacteria and colloids but not dissolved salts. Where TDS also needs reducing, UF is used ahead of RO as pre-treatment.

Frequently asked questions

Why does a UF plant need such frequent backwashing?

UF is a surface filtration process, so a cake layer builds up on the membrane within minutes and flow falls. Hollow fibre construction allows flow to be reversed to lift that cake off, typically every twenty to sixty minutes.

How long does a UF backwash cycle take?

One to two minutes in total — around thirty to sixty seconds of reverse flow, then twenty to forty seconds of forward flush, then drain. Plant availability therefore remains high.

What is CEB and how is it different from CIP?

Chemically enhanced backwash adds chemical to the normal backwash water, typically daily or every few days, to deal with organic or mineral fouling. A CIP is a full cleaning with a longer soak, done monthly to quarterly.

What is the key parameter to monitor on a UF plant?

Transmembrane pressure. Rising TMP indicates fouling accumulating. Permeate turbidity is the other critical one, because a rise there means a fibre has broken and unfiltered water is passing through.

How much water does UF recover?

Typically ninety to ninety five percent, considerably better than RO. Backwash water can often be returned to the raw water tank for re-treatment, which improves recovery further.
