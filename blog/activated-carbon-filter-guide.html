Activated carbon filter — removing chlorine, smell and colour

Aqua Filtration System, Faridabad · 9+ years in water treatment · Updated October 2026

Short answer: An activated carbon filter (ACF) is an FRP vessel filled with granular activated carbon. It removes free chlorine, bad smell, colour and many organic compounds. It is placed after the sand / multigrade filter and before the softener or RO plant, mainly to protect RO membranes from chlorine damage.

What activated carbon removes

  • Free chlorine — the main reason it is used before RO. Thin-film RO membranes are damaged by chlorine.
  • Odour and taste — the "medicine" or rotten-egg smell in many supplies
  • Colour — yellowish tint from organic matter
  • Organic compounds — some pesticides and solvents

It does not remove TDS, hardness or bacteria. A carbon bed can in fact grow bacteria if water stands in it for days, so it should be backwashed regularly.

Iodine value — 550 vs 900

The iodine value (IV) tells you how much internal surface the carbon has. A higher number means more adsorption capacity per kilogram.

GradeIodine valueBest for
Standard550–600 mg/gChlorine removal before RO, general dechlorination
High activity850–900 mg/gSmell, colour and organics; drinking water; longer bed life
Which to buy: if your only problem is chlorine before an RO plant, 550–600 IV is enough. If you have smell, colour or want the bed to last longer, the 850–900 grade is better value even at a higher price.

How much carbon to load

Carbon is loaded to about 60 percent of the vessel height, leaving freeboard for backwash, usually over a small gravel support layer. Carbon weighs roughly 450–500 kg per cubic metre, which works out to approximately:

1354~35 kg

1865~75 kg

These are planning figures — the exact quantity depends on the media layout. See the FRP vessel size chart for vessel volumes.

When to replace the carbon

  • Chlorine is detected after the filter (check with a chlorine test kit)
  • The smell or colour returns
  • Typically every 12–18 months before RO, sooner with high chlorine or organic load

Backwashing cleans dirt from the bed, but it does not restore exhausted carbon — that needs fresh carbon.

Carbon filter or SMBS dosing?

Both remove chlorine before RO. A carbon filter needs no chemical and also removes smell and organics. SMBS dosing is cheaper to set up on large plants but removes chlorine only. Many plants use carbon, with SMBS as a backup.

Frequently asked questions

What does an activated carbon filter remove?

Free chlorine, odour, colour and many organic compounds. It does not remove TDS, hardness or bacteria.

What is the difference between iodine value 550 and 900 carbon?

Iodine value measures adsorption capacity. 550 to 600 grade is enough for chlorine removal before RO; 850 to 900 grade removes smell, colour and organics better and lasts longer.

How often should activated carbon be changed?

Usually every 12 to 18 months before an RO plant, or as soon as chlorine, smell or colour appears in the filtered water.

Why is a carbon filter needed before RO?

Because free chlorine damages thin-film composite RO membranes. The carbon bed removes chlorine before water reaches the membrane.

Does backwashing regenerate activated carbon?

No. Backwash only removes trapped dirt. Exhausted carbon has to be replaced.
