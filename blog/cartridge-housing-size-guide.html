10 inch, 20 inch or jumbo — cartridge housing size guide

Aqua Filtration System, Faridabad · 9+ years in water treatment · Updated September 2026

Short answer: Choose the housing by flow, not by plant capacity. As a guide, a 10 inch slim housing handles 500 to 1,000 LPH, a 20 inch slim 1,000 to 2,000 LPH, and a jumbo (big blue) roughly double that.

Above that, multi-round SS housings are used, holding several cartridges together.

These are safe ranges. Higher flow will physically pass, but pressure drop increases and the filter chokes faster. Do not run at the limit — a slightly larger housing works out cheaper.

Matching the NB connection

The housing NB should match your line:

  • 15NB (½") — small plants, up to 500 LPH
  • 20NB (¾") — the most common, 1,000 to 2,000 LPH
  • 25NB (1") — 2,000 to 4,000 LPH
  • 40NB (1½") — above 4,000 LPH

The full NB chart is in our NB size guide — the same principles apply here.

Brass insert versions

The 20NB with brass inserts (₹682–761) has brass moulded into the threads. The advantage is that repeated opening does not wear the plastic thread.

When it is worth it: where cartridges are changed weekly or fortnightly, plain PP threads wear quickly.

Frequently asked questions

What is the difference between a 10 inch and 20 inch housing?

A twenty inch housing handles roughly double the flow, and the cartridge also lasts longer because there is more filter area. Where space permits, the twenty inch is the better choice.

When should I choose a jumbo or big blue housing?

When flow is higher and you do not want to change cartridges frequently. A jumbo cartridge is thicker so it holds considerably more dirt and lasts much longer.

Why should I take the air release version?

After changing a cartridge, air becomes trapped in the housing and reduces flow. An air release valve lets that air out easily. The price difference is only a few rupees, so it is always worth taking.

Should I choose PP or SS304?

For a general plant PP is adequate and much cheaper. Choose SS where pressure is higher, water is hot, or the plant runs continuously, because PP becomes brittle with age and sun exposure.

When is a bag filter housing better?

When the water is very dirty. A bag filter holds considerably more dirt than a cartridge, so it lasts much longer and reduces the effort of changing.
