Dosing pump will not prime — seven fixes

Aqua Filtration System, Faridabad · 9+ years in water treatment · Updated September 2026

Short answer: The pump is running but no chemical is moving — and there are three usual causes: air in the suction line, dirt or crystals in a valve, or the stroke set too low. All three are fixed at no cost.

Work through these seven checks before buying a new pump or calling for service.

First, confirm the chemical really is not moving

The simplest check is the tank level. Mark it and look again 24 hours later. If the level has not dropped, nothing is being dosed.

A second method: take the suction pipe out of the tank into a measuring jar, run for 10 minutes and see how many millilitres are drawn. This also confirms your actual dosing rate.

Cause 1: Air in the suction line

The most common cause. A diaphragm pump struggles to clear air, particularly when the chemical level in the tank is low.

Fix:

  1. Set the pump to 100 percent stroke
  2. Open the air release or bleed valve on the discharge side
  3. Run the pump until chemical flows without bubbles
  4. Close the bleed valve, then return the stroke to its setting
To stop it recurring: mount the tank above the pump so chemical feeds to it by gravity. This is called flooded suction, and it eliminates around 80 percent of priming problems.

Cause 2: Dirt or crystals in the foot valve

The foot valve sits at the end of the suction pipe. Antiscalant or hypo can dry and crystallise on it, or debris from the bottom of the tank can lodge in it.

Fix: remove the foot valve and soak it in warm water for 15 minutes, then clean it. For crystals use a mild acidic solution. Replace the strainer if it is torn.

Cause 3: A pump valve stuck

The pump head has two valves, suction and discharge, each containing a small ball or cone that moves back and forth. Dried chemical can seize them.

Fix: undo the pump head nuts, remove the valves and clean them in water or a mild acid. When refitting, note the orientation — fitted the wrong way round the pump will not work at all.

Safety: switch off the supply and release the chemical pressure before opening the pump. When opening an acid pump, always wear gloves and eye protection.

Cause 4: Stroke set too low

Below about 5 percent stroke a pump may not be able to prime at all. This often happens when the solution has been made up too concentrated.

Fix: prime at 100 percent first, then return to your setting. If your normal setting genuinely is below 5 percent, dilute the solution — the full method is in our dosing calculation guide.

Cause 5: Diaphragm torn or swollen

Signs of this: chemical weeping from under the pump, or a change in the sound it makes.

The cause is usually the wrong material — a PP pump on an acid line. See our PVDF vs PP guide.

Fix: the diaphragm has to be replaced. If it is an acid line, move to a PVDF model instead, or it will keep happening.

Cause 6: Discharge pressure too high

If the line pressure exceeds the pump rating, nothing will go in. For example a 4 kg/cm² pump on an 8 kg line.

Fix: check the line pressure with a gauge. If it is too high you need the Edose HP (10 kg/cm²), or move the injection point to a lower pressure location.

Cause 7: Suction line too long or too narrow

A diaphragm pump has limited suction lift. As a general rule:

  • Suction lift no more than 1.5 metres
  • Keep the suction pipe short and straight
  • Do not reduce the pipe size — a narrow pipe restricts suction

Four ways to prevent it

  1. Mount the tank above the pump — the single most effective step
  2. Fit a level switch (₹1,050) — the pump stops before the tank empties, so air never enters
  3. Check the tank level weekly — problems are caught early
  4. Do not keep solution beyond 15 to 20 days — old solution forms crystals and deposits

Frequently asked questions

How do I prime a dosing pump?

Set the pump to full stroke, open the bleed valve on the discharge side and run it until chemical flows without bubbles. Then close the bleed valve and return the stroke to its normal setting.

The pump is running but no chemical is going in — what should I check?

First confirm it really is not dosing by marking the tank level. Then check three things: air in the suction line, dirt in the foot valve and a stuck valve in the pump head. Most problems are found among those three.

Should the dosing tank be mounted above the pump?

Yes, this is the best arrangement. Chemical then feeds to the pump by gravity and priming problems largely disappear. It is known as flooded suction and should be done on every new installation.

Chemical is weeping from under the pump — what does that mean?

Usually the diaphragm has torn or swollen. The most common cause is the wrong material, such as a standard PP pump on an acid line. The diaphragm needs replacing, and on an acid line you should move to a PVDF model.

How long can the suction line be?

Suction lift should not exceed about one and a half metres, and the pipe should be as short and straight as possible. A long or narrow suction pipe makes it difficult for the pump to draw, and priming is repeatedly lost.
