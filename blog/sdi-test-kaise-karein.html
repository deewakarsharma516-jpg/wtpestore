SDI test for RO feed water — how to do it and what the result means

Aqua Filtration System, Faridabad · 9+ years in water treatment · Updated October 2026

Short answer: The Silt Density Index (SDI) tells you how quickly fine particles in the feed water will foul an RO membrane. You pass water through a 0.45 micron filter paper at 30 psi and time how fast it blocks. Membrane makers want SDI below 3 (ideally below 2.5); above 5, the RO should not be run without better pre-treatment.

Why SDI matters more than turbidity

Water can look perfectly clear — low NTU — and still carry colloids that coat an RO membrane within weeks. SDI catches these. A high SDI is one of the most common reasons for RO output dropping and for frequent CIP cleaning.

What you need

  • An SDI kit — filter holder, pressure regulator, gauge and valve
  • 0.45 micron filter papers (one per test)
  • A 500 ml measuring cylinder and a stopwatch
  • A sample point after the cartridge filter, before the high pressure pump

Step-by-step SDI test (SDI 15)

  1. Flush the sample line for a few minutes to clear stagnant water.
  2. Place a fresh 0.45 micron paper in the holder with tweezers, shiny side up. Do not touch it with fingers.
  3. Set the regulator to 30 psi (2.1 kg/cm²) and keep it constant through the test.
  4. Open the valve and measure the time to collect the first 500 ml — this is T1.
  5. Let water keep flowing for 15 minutes from the start.
  6. At 15 minutes, measure the time to collect another 500 ml — this is T2.
  7. Calculate: SDI = (1 − T1/T2) × 100 ÷ 15
Example: T1 = 20 seconds, T2 = 40 seconds → (1 − 20/40) × 100 ÷ 15 = SDI 3.3. That is above 3, so pre-treatment needs improving.

If the paper blocks so fast that you cannot collect 500 ml at 15 minutes, repeat at 5 or 10 minutes and divide by 5 or 10 instead.

Reading the result

SDIMeaningAction
Below 3Good RO feedNormal operation
3 – 5Fouling riskImprove MGF backwash, check cartridge, consider UF
Above 5Not suitable for ROAdd or upgrade pre-treatment before running RO

How to bring SDI down

  • Backwash the multigrade filter properly and on time
  • Use a 5 micron (or 1 micron) cartridge after the MGF
  • For surface water or treated effluent, add a UF plant — UF usually brings SDI below 3
  • Use a coagulant ahead of the sand filter for very fine colloids

Frequently asked questions

What SDI is acceptable for an RO plant?

Below 3 is acceptable and below 2.5 is ideal. Above 5 the RO should not be run without improving pre-treatment.

How is SDI calculated?

SDI = (1 − T1/T2) × 100 ÷ 15, where T1 is the time to collect the first 500 ml and T2 the time to collect 500 ml after 15 minutes, at a constant 30 psi through a 0.45 micron paper.

Where should the SDI sample be taken?

After the cartridge filter and before the high pressure pump — the water that actually reaches the membrane.

Can water with low turbidity still have high SDI?

Yes. Fine colloids that do not show as turbidity can still foul a membrane, which is why SDI is tested separately.

How do I reduce a high SDI?

Improve sand filter backwash, use a finer cartridge, add coagulant for colloids, or install a UF plant ahead of the RO.
