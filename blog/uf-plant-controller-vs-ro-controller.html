UF plant controller vs RO controller — what is different

Aqua Filtration System, Faridabad · 9+ years in water treatment · Updated September 2026

Short answer: An RO controller exists to run and protect pumps. A UF controller has a different job — it must run a backwash cycle repeatedly, automatically, in a fixed sequence. That is why a UF controller includes valve sequencing, which an RO controller does not have.

For this reason an RO panel will not properly run a UF plant, and the reverse is equally true.

Why the two jobs differ

An RO plant runs continuously

An RO plant runs for hours at a stretch. The controller only has to confirm the pumps are running properly, whether the tank is full, and that no protection has tripped.

A UF plant stops frequently

A UF membrane fouls quickly, so it has to be backwashed every 20 to 60 minutes. Each backwash opens and closes several valves in a fixed order:

  1. Filtration stops
  2. The backwash valve opens and water is sent in reverse
  3. Forward flush
  4. Drain
  5. Filtration resumes

This whole cycle must run automatically and on time — that is the real work of a UF controller.

Can an RO panel run a UF plant?

Technically to a limited extent, but not in practice, because:

  • Backwash timing would have to be done manually, several times a day
  • If the valve sequence goes wrong, reverse pressure can reach the membrane
  • If somebody forgets, the UF membrane fouls

A UF membrane costs considerably more than a controller, so economising here is expensive.

If you have both plants

Many sites have UF followed by RO, with UF as pre-treatment. There are two approaches:

  1. Two separate panels — UF NXT I for the UF and 33NXT for the RO. Simple, and if one fails the other keeps running
  2. One larger panel — such as the UF NXT I PLUS handling both

Our advice is two separate panels. It costs a little more but troubleshooting becomes far easier, and a fault in one does not stop the entire plant.

Frequently asked questions

What is the difference between a UF controller and an RO controller?

An RO controller mainly runs and protects pumps. A UF controller must in addition run the entire backwash sequence automatically, opening and closing several valves at set times, which is why it has more timers and contactors.

Can an RO panel be used on a UF plant?

Not in practice. An RO panel has no backwash sequencing, so the backwash would have to be done manually several times a day. Forgetting even once can foul the UF membrane, which costs far more than the controller.

How often does a UF plant backwash?

Typically every twenty to sixty minutes, depending on feed water quality. Dirtier water needs more frequent backwashing and cleaner water less. The interval is set in the controller.

What does the ROMATIC controller do?

It takes a conductivity sensor which continuously monitors permeate TDS. If the TDS rises it stops or diverts the plant so that poor quality water does not reach the tank. This is important in pharma, food and packaged water applications.

With both UF and RO, should I use one panel or two?

Two separate panels is generally better. It costs a little more but troubleshooting is much easier, and a problem with one panel does not bring the whole plant to a stop.
