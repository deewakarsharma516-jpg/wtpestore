STP technologies — MBBR, SBR and conventional compared

Aqua Filtration System, Faridabad · 9+ years in water treatment · Updated September 2026

Short answer: All three use bacteria to treat sewage; the difference is how the bacteria are held and how the process is arranged. MBBR grows bacteria on floating plastic media, SBR does everything in one tank in timed phases, and conventional activated sludge uses separate tanks in a continuous flow.

For a building or housing society with limited space, MBBR or SBR is usually chosen. Conventional plants need more land but are simpler to operate.

Conventional activated sludge

The traditional arrangement — separate tanks for each stage, with continuous flow through them:

Screening → equalisation → aeration → clarifier → filtration → disinfection

AdvantageLimitation
Simple, well understoodNeeds the most land
Easy to operateSensitive to load variation
Lower capital cost per tankLarger civil works

MBBR — Moving Bed Biofilm Reactor

Plastic carrier media floats in the aeration tank, and bacteria grow as a biofilm on the media surface rather than suspended in the water.

AdvantageLimitation
Compact — much less spaceMedia cost
Handles load variation wellMedia can be lost if screens fail
High biomass in a small tankRequires good aeration for media movement
No sludge return neededMore complex than conventional

Why it handles variation well: the biofilm stays attached to the media, so it cannot wash out during a high flow period the way suspended biomass can.

SBR — Sequencing Batch Reactor

Everything happens in one tank, in timed phases rather than in separate tanks:

  1. Fill — sewage enters
  2. React — aeration, bacteria consume the load
  3. Settle — aeration off, biomass settles
  4. Decant — clear water drawn off the top
  5. Idle — ready for the next cycle
AdvantageLimitation
No separate clarifier neededRequires reliable automation
Compact footprintBatch process, needs buffering
Good nutrient removal possibleMore instrumentation
Flexible cycle timingDependent on the control panel
An SBR depends entirely on its control panel. If the sequencing fails, the whole plant stops. So panel quality and having spares matters more here than in other designs.

The electricity cost

In every design, the blower dominates the electricity bill — typically 60 to 70 percent of consumption. So DO monitoring is worthwhile regardless of technology.

In an SBR there is an additional saving available, because aeration is off during the settle and decant phases. Getting the cycle timing right therefore matters commercially as well as technically.

Treated water reuse

Most STPs now feed the treated water to:

  • Gardening and landscaping
  • Toilet flushing
  • Cooling tower make-up
  • Floor and road washing

For reuse the tertiary stage matters — filtration and disinfection, and sometimes RO. Outlet quality monitoring becomes important because the water is going somewhere people encounter it.

Automation

All three need a control panel to sequence pumps and blowers, with SBR being the most dependent on it. Panel selection is covered in our ETP/STP panel guide.

Frequently asked questions

What is the difference between MBBR and SBR?

MBBR grows bacteria as a biofilm on floating plastic media in a continuous flow arrangement. SBR does everything in one tank in timed phases of fill, react, settle and decant, so it needs no separate clarifier.

Which STP technology needs the least space?

MBBR generally needs the least, because the plastic media holds a high concentration of biomass in a small tank. SBR is also compact since it needs no separate clarifier. Conventional activated sludge needs the most land.

Why does MBBR handle load variation well?

The biofilm stays attached to the carrier media, so it cannot wash out during a period of high flow the way suspended biomass can in a conventional plant.

What is the main risk with an SBR?

It depends entirely on its control panel for sequencing. If the automation fails, the whole plant stops. Panel quality and keeping spares matter more here than with other designs.

What dominates the electricity cost of an STP?

The blower, typically sixty to seventy percent of consumption, regardless of which technology is used. That is why dissolved oxygen monitoring is worthwhile in every design.
