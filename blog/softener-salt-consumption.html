How much salt does a softener use — and how to reduce it

Aqua Filtration System, Faridabad · 9+ years in water treatment · Updated September 2026

Short answer: A softener uses roughly 100 to 150 grams of salt per litre of resin per regeneration. So a 1465 vessel with 98 litres of resin needs about 12 kg each time. Using more than this brings little extra benefit, because the resin can only exchange so much per cycle.

Working out the monthly cost

Multiply salt per cycle by the number of regenerations per month.

Example: a 1665 vessel regenerating every 8 days:

  • Regenerations per month: about 4
  • Salt: 15 kg × 4 = 60 kg a month

This is worth working out before ordering, because it is a recurring cost that continues for the life of the plant.

Why more salt does not mean better

The resin has a fixed number of exchange sites. Once they are all reloaded with sodium, additional salt simply passes through to drain.

There is a genuine trade-off here: a lower salt dose is more efficient per kilogram but gives less capacity, so you regenerate more often. A higher dose gives more capacity per cycle but wastes salt. The 100 to 150 gram range is where most installations sit.

Salt quality

  • Use salt intended for softeners — clean, low in insolubles
  • Avoid agricultural or road salt, which carries impurities that settle in the tank and eventually reach the resin
  • Tablet or coarse salt is preferable to fine powder, which tends to bridge and crust
  • Cheap dirty salt costs more in the long run through resin fouling and tank cleaning

Six ways to reduce salt consumption

  1. Regenerate on volume, not on days — an automatic valve set on volume avoids regenerating when little water has been used. See our EVOLVE guide
  2. Do not over-dose — more than 150 g per litre of resin is largely wasted
  3. Fix the brine draw — if brine is drawn too fast, salt is used without full regeneration
  4. Size the softener correctly — an oversized vessel regenerating on a fixed schedule wastes salt
  5. Prevent channelling — if part of the bed is unused, you are regenerating resin that never worked. See our distribution guide
  6. Break up salt bridges — if salt is not dissolving, you regenerate with weak brine and get poor capacity, then regenerate again sooner

Signs that salt is being wasted

  • Salty taste in the first water after regeneration — rinse too short, or dose too high
  • Salt consumption rising without any change in water usage
  • Capacity falling while salt use stays the same — the regeneration is not working properly
  • Sludge in the brine tank — poor salt quality

Brine tank maintenance

  • Keep salt above the water level so saturated brine is always available
  • Check for bridging — push a stick down through the salt to feel for a void
  • Clean annually — sludge accumulates at the bottom over time
  • Keep it covered — dirt and insects otherwise end up on the resin
  • Site it close to the valve — a long brine line restricts draw

Estimating the annual cost

Take the 1665 example again — 60 kg a month is 720 kg a year. At local salt prices you can work out the annual figure directly, and it is usually a bigger number than people expect.

That is why correct sizing matters financially, not just technically. A softener sized properly and regenerating on volume can use noticeably less salt than one that is oversized and running on a fixed timer.

Frequently asked questions

How much salt does a softener use per regeneration?

Roughly one hundred to one hundred and fifty grams per litre of resin. A 1465 vessel holding about ninety eight litres of resin therefore needs around twelve kilograms each time.

Does using more salt give better results?

Only up to a point. The resin has a fixed number of exchange sites, so beyond about one hundred and fifty grams per litre the extra salt largely passes to drain without any benefit.

What type of salt should be used?

Salt intended for softeners, which is clean and low in insoluble matter. Avoid agricultural or road salt, whose impurities settle in the tank and eventually foul the resin. Tablet or coarse salt is preferable to fine powder.

How can I reduce salt consumption?

Regenerate on volume rather than on a fixed number of days, avoid over-dosing, make sure the brine draw is working properly, size the softener correctly, and prevent channelling so the whole resin bed is actually being used.

Why is my salt consumption increasing?

Either the regeneration is not working properly so capacity has fallen and you are regenerating more often, or the brine draw rate is wrong, or salt is bridging in the tank so weak brine is being made.
