ETP stages explained — from raw effluent to discharge

Aqua Filtration System, Faridabad · 9+ years in water treatment · Updated September 2026

Short answer: An ETP works in three broad stages — primary removes solids physically and chemically, secondary uses bacteria to consume the organic load, and tertiary polishes the water to meet discharge standards.

Most ETP failures are in the secondary stage, because that is the one that depends on living bacteria, and bacteria are sensitive to pH, oxygen and shock loads.

Primary treatment

Screening

Coarse solids, rags and debris are removed by bar screens. Simple, but essential — anything that gets past here damages pumps downstream.

Oil and grease trap

Floating oil is skimmed off. Oil passing into the aeration tank coats the bacteria and stops them working.

Equalisation tank

This is more important than it looks. Effluent arrives in batches with varying pH and strength; the equalisation tank evens it out so the biological stage receives a steady load.

An undersized equalisation tank is a common design fault. Without adequate buffering, a shock load of strong or acidic effluent reaches the aeration tank directly and can kill the biomass — after which the plant takes weeks to recover.

Neutralisation

pH is corrected to 6.5 to 8.5, because bacteria will not work outside that range. Acid or alkali is dosed automatically using a pH controller with ETP sensor (₹28,350) and a dosing pump.

See our pH correction guide.

Coagulation and flocculation

Chemicals are dosed to make fine suspended particles clump together into larger flocs that will settle.

Primary clarifier

The flocs settle out as sludge and are removed.

Secondary treatment — the biological stage

This is where the dissolved organic load (BOD and COD) is actually removed, by bacteria consuming it.

Aeration tank

A blower supplies air so the bacteria have oxygen. Target dissolved oxygen is 1.5 to 3 mg/L.

  • Below 1 mg/L — bacteria weaken, treatment fails
  • Above 4 mg/L — no benefit, just wasted electricity

The blower typically consumes 60 to 70 percent of the plant's electricity, so DO monitoring pays for itself. See our DO meter guide.

Secondary clarifier

Biomass settles out. Part is returned to the aeration tank to maintain the bacterial population; the surplus is removed as waste sludge.

Sludge return is critical. If it stops, the bacterial population in the aeration tank washes out and treatment fails within days.

Tertiary treatment

  • Sand or multigrade filter — removes remaining suspended solids
  • Activated carbon filter — removes colour and residual organics
  • Disinfection — chlorination or UV
  • RO — where the water is to be reused

Where ETPs most commonly fail

  1. pH outside range — bacteria stop working; everything downstream then fails
  2. Shock load — a strong or toxic batch kills the biomass
  3. Inadequate DO — blower undersized or DO sensor dirty and reading falsely
  4. Sludge return stopped — biomass washes out
  5. Low MLSS — too few bacteria for the load
  6. Oil reaching the aeration tank — coats the biomass
  7. Undersized equalisation — no buffering against variation

The 30 minute settling test is simple and very informative. Fill a measuring cylinder with mixed liquor, let it settle for 30 minutes, and note the sludge volume. Poor settling indicates bulking, which usually points to a DO or nutrient problem.

Recovering after a biomass failure

If the bacteria have been killed, recovery takes time:

  1. Identify and fix the cause first — otherwise it happens again
  2. Correct pH and DO
  3. Seed with biomass from a working plant if available
  4. Feed the plant gradually, at reduced load, while the population rebuilds
  5. Monitor MLSS as it recovers

This typically takes two to four weeks, which is why prevention matters so much more than cure here.

Frequently asked questions

What are the main stages of an ETP?

Primary treatment removes solids physically and chemically, secondary treatment uses bacteria to consume the organic load, and tertiary treatment polishes the water to meet discharge standards.

Why is the equalisation tank important?

Effluent arrives in batches with varying pH and strength, and the equalisation tank evens it out. Without adequate buffering a shock load can reach the aeration tank directly and kill the biomass, after which recovery takes weeks.

Why do most ETP failures happen in the secondary stage?

Because it depends on living bacteria, which are sensitive to pH, oxygen levels and shock loads. If pH goes outside six point five to eight point five, or dissolved oxygen falls too low, the bacteria stop working.

Why does sludge return matter?

Part of the settled biomass is returned to the aeration tank to maintain the bacterial population. If the return stops, the population washes out and treatment fails within days.

How long does it take to recover after the biomass dies?

Typically two to four weeks. The cause must be fixed first, then pH and DO corrected, the plant seeded if possible, and fed gradually at reduced load while the population rebuilds.
