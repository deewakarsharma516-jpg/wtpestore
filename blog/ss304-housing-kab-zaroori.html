When an SS304 housing is necessary — upgrading from PP

Aqua Filtration System, Faridabad · 9+ years in water treatment · Updated September 2026

Short answer: An SS304 housing is needed where pressure is high, water is hot, the plant runs continuously, or the application is food or pharma. On a general RO plant a PP housing (₹236 to ₹651) is entirely adequate, and SS (from ₹20,475) is unnecessary expense there.

When SS304 is genuinely needed

1. Pressure above 7 kg/cm²

PP is fine up to about 7 kg. Beyond that it can distort or fail. On a high pressure line SS is the safe choice.

2. Hot water above 45°C

PP softens in hot water and loses strength. On boiler feed or hot CIP water, SS is essential.

3. Continuously running plants

On a plant running 24 hours, a PP housing typically needs replacing every year or two. SS lasts ten years, so over time it becomes the cheaper option.

4. Food, pharma and dairy

Here the material has to be food grade and capable of steam sanitisation, which PP cannot do.

5. Installed outdoors or in sunlight

PP becomes brittle in sunlight and cracks within a year or two.

For multi-round arrangements there are also clamp and flanged fitting options, from ₹8,400 and ₹30,450 respectively.

Clamp or flanged

  • Clamp: opens quickly, suited to small and medium sizes. Better where cartridges are changed frequently
  • Flanged: more robust, for larger sizes and higher pressures, but slower to open

Flanged versions are considerably more expensive, so choose them only where size or pressure genuinely demands it.

On direct cost PP is still cheaper, even over ten years. So the decision on SS should be made on need rather than cost:

  • High pressure or temperature → SS (a safety question)
  • Food or pharma → SS (a regulatory question)
  • The plant cannot be allowed to stop → SS (a reliability question)
  • General RO plant → choose PP

SS304 vs SS316

There is a further grade above SS304:

  • SS304: suitable for normal water, RO and food work
  • SS316: for saline water, chemicals and seawater — more corrosion resistant, and more expensive

SS304 is adequate for most water treatment work. SS316 is needed where chloride levels are high, as in seawater or brine.

Points to consider when choosing SS

  1. The cartridge stays the same — an SS housing takes the same standard cartridge
  2. It is heavy — the support or bracket must be adequate
  3. Connection type — threaded, clamp or flanged, to suit your line
  4. Gauge ports — SS housings usually provide for pressure gauges, and they should be fitted

The straightforward answer

If you are wondering whether to buy SS and your plant is a general RO system running at normal pressure and temperature — choose PP. Put the money saved into good cartridges, pressure gauges and spare O-rings, which will serve you better.

Frequently asked questions

When should an SS304 housing be used?

Where pressure exceeds about seven kg per cm2, where the water is hot, where the plant runs continuously, or in food and pharma applications. On a general RO plant a PP housing is entirely adequate and far cheaper.

How long does a PP housing last?

Two to four years in normal service. In direct sunlight it becomes brittle sooner, and on a continuously running plant it may need replacing every one to two years.

Is SS304 cheaper in the long run?

On direct cost, no — PP remains cheaper even over ten years. The decision to use SS should be based on need, such as pressure, temperature or regulatory requirements, rather than on cost.

What is the difference between SS304 and SS316?

SS316 is more corrosion resistant and is needed for saline water, chemicals or seawater. For most water treatment work SS304 is adequate and less expensive.

Does an SS housing need a different cartridge?

No, it takes the same standard cartridge as a PP housing. Only the housing material and strength differ; the cartridge size and micron rating remain the same.
