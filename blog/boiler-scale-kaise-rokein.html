Boiler scale — why it forms and how to prevent it

Aqua Filtration System, Faridabad · 9+ years in water treatment · Updated September 2026

Short answer: Scale forms because boiling concentrates the dissolved salts in the water. As water evaporates the salts stay behind, and once they exceed their solubility they deposit on the hottest surfaces — the tubes.

Scale is a poor conductor of heat, so even a thin layer raises fuel consumption measurably and causes local overheating of tube metal.

Why boilers scale so readily

In a boiler, water leaves as steam but the dissolved salts do not. So the concentration in the boiler water rises continuously as more make-up is added.

Two things then drive deposition:

  • Concentration — salts exceed their solubility limit
  • Temperature — calcium carbonate becomes less soluble as temperature rises, so it deposits preferentially on the hottest surfaces
That second point is the problem. Scale forms exactly where you least want it — on the hottest tube surfaces, where it then causes local overheating.

What scale costs

  • Higher fuel consumption — heat has to pass through an insulating layer
  • Local overheating of the tube metal beneath the deposit
  • Tube failure in severe cases
  • Descaling shutdowns — lost production plus chemical cleaning cost
  • Reduced steam output from the same firing rate

The fuel penalty alone is usually enough to justify a full treatment programme.

Three lines of defence

1. Remove hardness before the boiler

This is the primary defence. A softener on the make-up water removes calcium and magnesium before they ever reach the boiler.

Sizing is covered in our softener sizing guide. For higher pressure boilers, DM water or RO permeate may be required instead.

2. Chemical treatment inside the boiler

Even softened water carries some residual hardness and other salts. Boiler antiscalant keeps these dispersed as a sludge that can be removed by blowdown, rather than allowing them to bake onto the tubes.

Alongside it, alkalinity and pH control keeps the boiler water in the range where the treatment chemistry works and corrosion is minimised.

3. Blowdown

Blowdown removes concentrated boiler water and replaces it with fresh feed, keeping the dissolved solids within limits.

  • Too little blowdown — solids concentrate and scale forms
  • Too much blowdown — heat and treated water are wasted

The right amount is judged by conductivity of the boiler water, which is why a conductivity meter is standard on boiler systems. See our conductivity guide.

How blowdown control works

Boiler water conductivity is monitored, and blowdown is adjusted to hold it below the limit for that boiler pressure. Higher pressure boilers require tighter limits.

A conductivity controller with temperature compensation (₹7,980) is appropriate here, because boiler water is hot and an uncompensated reading would be misleading.

Signs of scaling

  • Fuel consumption rising for the same steam output
  • Flue gas temperature rising
  • Steam output falling
  • Deposits visible during inspection
  • Boiler water conductivity consistently high

What to monitor routinely

TestFrequency
Feed water hardnessDaily — with a test kit
Boiler water conductivityContinuous or daily
Boiler water pH and alkalinityDaily
Scavenger residualDaily
Condensate pH and ironWeekly
Feed water hardness is the one to watch most closely. If the softener fails and hard water reaches the boiler, scaling begins immediately. A daily hardness test takes two minutes and is the cheapest protection in the entire system.

If the softener fails

This is the most common route to boiler scaling — the softener stops working and nobody notices for weeks. Our softener troubleshooting guide covers the causes, but the practical protection is simply to test the feed hardness every day.

Frequently asked questions

Why does scale form in a boiler?

Water leaves as steam but dissolved salts stay behind, so the concentration rises continuously. Once salts exceed their solubility they deposit, and calcium carbonate becomes less soluble as temperature rises so it deposits on the hottest tube surfaces.

What does boiler scale cost?

Scale is a poor conductor of heat, so fuel consumption rises for the same steam output and flue gas temperature increases. It also causes local overheating of the tube metal, which in severe cases leads to tube failure.

What is the primary defence against boiler scale?

Removing hardness before the boiler with a softener on the make-up water. Chemical treatment and blowdown then deal with what remains, but they cannot compensate for hard water reaching the boiler continuously.

How is blowdown controlled?

By monitoring boiler water conductivity and adjusting blowdown to hold it below the limit for that boiler pressure. Too little blowdown allows solids to concentrate, while too much wastes heat and treated water.

What should be tested daily on a boiler?

Feed water hardness is the most important, because if the softener fails and hard water reaches the boiler, scaling begins immediately. Boiler water conductivity, pH and alkalinity, and scavenger residual should also be checked.
