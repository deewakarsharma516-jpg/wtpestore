Motorised vs solenoid dosing pump — which to choose

Aqua Filtration System, Faridabad · 9+ years in water treatment · Updated September 2026

Short answer: A solenoid pump covers 6 to 10 LPH — economical, compact, and adequate for around 90 percent of RO plants. A motorised pump starts at 25 LPH — more expensive, but built for heavy continuous duty.

Put simply: if your dosing falls within 10 LPH, take a solenoid and save the money. Above 25 LPH there is no alternative to motorised.

How each works

Solenoid — driven by an electromagnet

An electromagnet pushes the diaphragm and releases it. Each push is one stroke. You can set both strokes per minute and stroke length.

Advantages: compact, light, economical, very low power consumption, no rotating parts.

Limitation: the coil heats up under prolonged heavy duty, and capacity is limited.

Motorised — driven by a motor and gearbox

A motor drives the diaphragm through a gearbox. It can run continuously, 24 hours a day.

Advantages: high capacity (up to 400 LPH), continuous duty, more robust.

Limitations: expensive, larger, higher power consumption, and the gearbox oil needs attention.

Frequently asked questions

What is the difference between solenoid and motorised dosing pumps?

In a solenoid pump an electromagnet drives the diaphragm; these are compact, economical and go up to about ten LPH. A motorised pump uses a motor and gearbox, starts at around twenty five LPH and is built for heavy continuous duty.

Does an RO plant need a motorised pump?

Usually not. However large the plant, RO antiscalant dosing stays within solenoid range. Motorised pumps are really intended for ETP and STP work where much larger chemical volumes are required.

Do motorised pumps need more maintenance?

Slightly. Diaphragm and valves need attention on both types, but a motorised pump also has gearbox oil to check periodically. In return it is far more robust for continuous running.

Can a solenoid pump run continuously for 24 hours?

It can, but the coil warms up, so very prolonged heavy duty will shorten its life. For genuinely continuous heavy dosing a motorised pump is the better choice.

Why do MAX 50 and MAX 100 cost the same?

The basic build and motor are the same, with only the dosing head setting differing. So if there is any chance the requirement will grow, taking the larger model makes sense because it costs nothing extra.
