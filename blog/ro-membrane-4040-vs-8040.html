4040 vs 8040 RO membranes — sizing and output

Aqua Filtration System, Faridabad · 9+ years in water treatment · Updated September 2026

Short answer: The four digit code is the diameter and length in inches. A 4040 is 4 inches across and 40 inches long; an 8040 is 8 by 40. An 8040 has roughly four times the membrane area of a 4040, so it produces around four times the water.

The choice is driven by plant capacity — 4040 elements for smaller plants, 8040 for larger ones where fewer, bigger elements are more economical.

When to move from 4040 to 8040

The crossover is usually around 2,000 to 3,000 LPH. Above that, 8040 elements are generally better because:

  • Fewer elements to handle and replace
  • Fewer housings, connections and O-rings — so fewer leak points
  • Lower cost per litre of capacity
  • Less space for the same output

But 4040 has advantages too

  • Easier to handle — an 8040 element is heavy and awkward
  • Lower cost per element — replacement is less of a single hit
  • More flexible — capacity can be adjusted in smaller steps
  • Widely available — easier to source quickly

Most Indian borewell applications use brackish water elements. Low pressure types are worth considering where feed TDS is genuinely low, because they save pump energy.

Housings must match

An element needs a pressure vessel of matching diameter and length, rated for your operating pressure. Housings are available in various connection sizes — 25NB, 40NB and 50NB.

Also check:

  • Number of elements per housing — housings hold 1, 2, 3 or more elements in series
  • Pressure rating — must exceed your operating pressure with margin
  • End port or side port — depending on your piping layout

Elements in series

In a multi-element housing, water passes from one element to the next. The first element sees the freshest water and produces most; the last sees the most concentrated water and produces least.

This is why:

  • All elements should be replaced together — mixing old and new gives uneven performance
  • The last element is most vulnerable to scaling, since it sees the highest concentration
  • Reject flow matters most at the last element — see our flow monitoring guide

Frequently asked questions

What does 4040 mean on an RO membrane?

It is the diameter and length in inches. A 4040 element is four inches in diameter and forty inches long, while an 8040 is eight by forty inches and has about four times the membrane area.

How much water does a 4040 membrane produce?

Typically three hundred to four hundred litres per hour under normal brackish water conditions, though actual output depends on feed TDS, temperature and pressure. High TDS and cold water both reduce it significantly.

When should I move from 4040 to 8040 elements?

Usually around two to three thousand LPH. Above that, 8040 elements mean fewer housings and connections, lower cost per litre and less space, though individual elements are heavier and more expensive to replace.

Which membrane type do I need?

For most Indian borewell water a brackish water element is correct. Low pressure types suit genuinely low TDS feed and save pump energy, while seawater elements are only for very high TDS applications.

Should all elements in a housing be replaced together?

Yes. Water passes from one element to the next, so mixing old and new gives uneven performance. Replace the whole set together, along with the O-rings and brine seals.
