How to size a dosing tank — calculation and chart

Aqua Filtration System, Faridabad · 9+ years in water treatment · Updated September 2026

Short answer: The tank should hold enough solution for 7 to 15 days — no more. For small RO plants a 10 or 15 litre LLDPE vessel is sufficient. The problem with an oversized tank is that the solution ages and bacteria can develop in it.

The sizing formula

Tank (litres) = solution ml/hr × 24 × number of days ÷ 1,000
Then add 20 percent freeboard.

Example: 120 ml/hr dosing, 10 days of solution required:
120 × 24 × 10 ÷ 1,000 = 28.8 litres, plus 20 percent = around 35 litres

But if you make the solution more concentrated, the ml/hr falls and a smaller tank will do. That is why tank size and solution strength are decided together.

Notice that on the 1,000 LPH plant, moving from a 5 percent to a 10 percent solution doubles how long the same tank lasts. That is the simplest way to keep the tank small.

Why LLDPE

  • Chemically resistant — antiscalant, hypo and alkali are all fine
  • Moulded in one piece — no joints, so no leaks
  • UV stabilised — does not degrade in sunlight
  • Light and economical — and unlike an MS tank it cannot rust
What not to do: never use an old paint bucket or a food container as a dosing tank. It can be attacked by the chemical, and its material may react with the chemical and render the dosing useless.

Five rules for installing the tank

  1. Mount it above the pump so chemical feeds by gravity — this eliminates most priming problems and is the single most important rule.
  2. Keep it covered — dust, insects and sunlight all degrade the solution. Algae grows in an open tank.
  3. Make the level visible — a translucent tank or a marked scale on the outside. If you can see the level, you notice before it runs out.
  4. Fit a level switch — ₹1,050 protects both the pump and the membrane. Full details in our level switch guide.
  5. Mix properly — powder antiscalant settles at the bottom if not dissolved fully, which makes dosing inaccurate.

How long solution stays usable

ChemicalSolution life
Antiscalant15 – 20 days
Sodium hypochlorite7 – 10 days (loses strength quickly)
SMBS7 – 10 days
Caustic / acidLonger, but check the tank material

So when sizing the tank, take the chemical life into account too. There is no point making a 30 day tank of hypo when it loses strength within 10.

A common mistake — oversizing

People assume a bigger tank means less work. In fact:

  • Old solution loses strength — dosing continues but has little effect
  • Bacteria can develop, which are themselves harmful to the membrane
  • Sediment collects at the bottom and ends up choking the suction valve

The right approach is a smaller tank refilled more often with fresh solution. It is slightly more work but much better for the plant.

Frequently asked questions

What size dosing tank should I use?

One that holds enough solution for seven to fifteen days. For small RO plants a ten or fifteen litre LLDPE vessel is usually adequate. A larger tank means the solution sits too long and loses strength.

Should the tank be mounted above the pump?

Yes, this is the best arrangement. Chemical then feeds to the pump by gravity and priming problems largely disappear. It should be done on every new installation.

How long does an antiscalant solution last?

Fifteen to twenty days. Beyond that bacteria can develop in it, which are themselves harmful to the membrane. Sodium hypochlorite solution loses strength even faster, within seven to ten days.

Can I use an ordinary plastic drum as a dosing tank?

You should use an LLDPE vessel, which is made for chemical service and moulded in one piece so it does not leak. An old paint bucket or food container can be attacked by the chemical and may also react with it.

What should I watch when making up the solution?

Dissolve powder chemicals fully, otherwise they settle at the bottom and dosing becomes inaccurate. Keep the tank covered to protect the solution from dust and sunlight, and rinse the tank before each new batch.
