UF vs RO membranes — when to use which

Aqua Filtration System, Faridabad · 9+ years in water treatment · Updated September 2026

Short answer: UF removes suspended solids, bacteria and turbidity but leaves dissolved salts untouched — so TDS is unchanged. RO removes dissolved salts as well, reducing TDS by 95 percent or more.

So if your problem is cloudiness or bacteria, UF is enough and far cheaper to run. If the problem is high TDS or salty taste, only RO will help.

The recovery difference matters commercially. UF recovers 90 to 95 percent of the feed water, while RO typically recovers 50 to 75 percent. Where water itself is scarce or expensive, that difference is significant.

When UF alone is enough

  • Water is cloudy or turbid but the TDS is acceptable
  • Bacteriological safety is the concern rather than dissolved salts
  • River or surface water where TDS is low but turbidity is high
  • Pre-treatment ahead of another process
  • Where you want to retain the natural minerals in the water

When RO is necessary

  • TDS is high — water tastes salty or brackish
  • Hardness needs removing along with everything else
  • Nitrate, fluoride or arsenic are present
  • Boiler feed or process water requiring low conductivity
  • Packaged drinking water

UF as pre-treatment for RO

On difficult feed water, UF is often placed ahead of the RO. The UF removes turbidity and colloids so that the RO membranes see clean water.

The benefit: RO membranes last considerably longer, because the main cause of fouling has been removed upstream. On river or surface water this arrangement often pays for itself in membrane life alone.

UF plants need their own controller, because backwash sequencing is required — see our UF vs RO controller guide.

UF needs frequent backwashing

This is the main operational difference. A UF membrane fouls quickly at the surface and has to be backwashed every 20 to 60 minutes, automatically.

Each backwash cycle involves reverse flow, forward flush and drain, all sequenced by the controller. Options include the ASTERO UF NG (₹15,750), UF NXT I (₹23,100) and UFMATIC controller (₹17,850).

Membrane formats

UF membranes are usually hollow fibre — thousands of fine tubes bundled together, giving a very large surface area. RO membranes are spiral wound sheets.

This is why UF can be backwashed while RO generally cannot — reverse flow through a spiral wound element would damage it.

A frequent misunderstanding

People sometimes install UF expecting the TDS to fall, and are disappointed when it does not change at all. UF does not remove dissolved salts — the pores are far too large.

Equally, some install RO where UF would have been sufficient, and then pay for higher power consumption and lost reject water indefinitely.

Test the water first. The TDS figure alone tells you which of the two you actually need.

Frequently asked questions

What is the difference between UF and RO?

UF removes suspended solids, bacteria and turbidity but leaves dissolved salts untouched, so TDS is unchanged. RO removes dissolved salts as well and reduces TDS by ninety five percent or more.

Will UF reduce my TDS?

No. UF pores are far too large to retain dissolved salts, so the TDS after UF is the same as before. If the water tastes salty or the TDS is high, RO is what you need.

Why is UF cheaper to run than RO?

It operates at one to three kg per cm2 rather than ten to fifteen, so power consumption is much lower, and it recovers ninety to ninety five percent of the feed water against fifty to seventy five percent for RO.

Why is UF used ahead of RO?

On difficult feed water such as river or surface water, UF removes turbidity and colloids so the RO membranes see clean water. RO membranes then last considerably longer, which often pays for the UF stage.

Why does UF need frequent backwashing?

UF membranes foul quickly at the surface, so they are backwashed every twenty to sixty minutes. The hollow fibre construction allows reverse flow, whereas a spiral wound RO element would be damaged by it.
