pH controller PO 650 — installation and calibration

Aqua Filtration System, Faridabad · 9+ years in water treatment · Updated September 2026

Short answer: The PO 650 measures pH and can run a dosing pump through its relay. Three things matter for it to work properly — fitting the sensor away from the injection point, doing a proper two-point calibration, and setting a dead band so the pump does not switch on and off constantly.

Where to fit the sensor — the critical point

The most common mistake: fitting the sensor right next to the chemical injection point. There the water is not yet mixed, so the sensor alternately reads far too high and far too low, and the controller behaves erratically.

The correct approach:

  • At least 10 to 15 seconds of flow travel from the injection point
  • Where water is continuously moving, not stagnant
  • Where the sensor stays permanently immersed and never sees air
  • Easy to remove — calibration is a monthly job
A practical arrangement: build a small bypass loop for the sensor with isolating valves. Then calibration does not require shutting the plant down — just close the loop and take the sensor out.

Installation step by step

  1. Mount the controller in a panel, away from sun and water, at eye level
  2. Fit the sensor tee into the line, usually 15NB or 20NB
  3. Run the cable — keep the sensor cable away from power cables, or noise will appear in the reading
  4. Apply power — the display should come on
  5. Calibrate (see below)
  6. Set the setpoint and dead band
  7. Wire the relay to the dosing pump
  8. Test — use buffer solution to confirm the pump starts and stops

Two-point calibration

  1. Remove the sensor, rinse with clean water, blot dry gently
  2. Place in buffer 7 and let it settle for a minute or two
  3. Enter calibration mode and set 7.00
  4. Rinse, then place in buffer 4 (for acid side work) or buffer 9.2 (for alkaline side)
  5. Set that value
  6. Refit the sensor

Which second buffer: use buffer 4 if you dose acid, and 9.2 if you dose alkali — the side you actually work on.

Dead band — do not skip this

If your target is pH 7.0 and you simply set 7.0, the pump will start at 6.99 and stop at 7.01 — hundreds of times over. Both pump and relay will fail early.

So set a dead band: for example start at 6.8 and stop at 7.2. Now the pump only runs when it is genuinely needed.

Wiring the relay to the pump

The PO 650 relay acts as a switch. Two approaches:

  • Direct to the pump — the relay carries the pump supply. Simple, and fine for small pumps
  • Through a contactor — for larger pumps, the relay drives a contactor which drives the pump. This is safer

Maintenance schedule

  • Weekly: glance at the reading for any sudden change
  • Monthly: two-point calibration
  • Quarterly: clean the sensor, check cable and connections
  • Annually: assess sensor condition and arrange a spare

Frequently asked questions

Where should a pH sensor be fitted?

At least ten to fifteen seconds of flow travel away from the chemical injection point, where the water is thoroughly mixed. Fitted too close, the sensor gives a false reading and the controller cannot work properly.

What is a dead band and why does it matter?

A dead band is a range, such as starting at six point eight and stopping at seven point two. Without it the pump switches on and off hundreds of times around the target value, which quickly wears out both pump and relay.

How often should a pH controller be calibrated?

At least once a month, using the two-point method with buffer seven followed by buffer four or nine point two. Calibration also tells you how the sensor is holding up.

What is the difference between PO 350 and PO 650?

The PO 350 only displays pH, while the PO 650 has a relay so it can operate a dosing pump. If you only need to see the reading the PO 350 is cheaper, but for automatic control you need the PO 650.

The reading is unstable — what should I check?

First check whether the sensor cable runs near a power cable, as that introduces noise. Then check the earthing, and confirm the sensor is not fitted too close to the injection point.
