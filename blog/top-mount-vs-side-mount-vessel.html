Top mount vs side mount vessel — which to choose

Aqua Filtration System, Faridabad · 9+ years in water treatment · Updated September 2026

Short answer: On a top mount vessel the valve sits on the top opening, which is the usual arrangement for smaller vessels and needs headroom above. On a side mount the valve is fitted to a side opening, which suits larger vessels and works where headroom is limited.

The choice affects not just the vessel but also which multiport valve you can use, so it must be decided before ordering.

The difference

Top mount

The vessel has a threaded opening on top, and the valve screws directly into it. The riser pipe runs up the centre of the vessel into the valve.

Side mount

The vessel has a flanged opening on the side, usually near the top, and the valve bolts on there. The internal piping is arranged accordingly.

When to choose top mount

  • Vessel size up to about 2160
  • There is headroom above the vessel
  • You want the more economical option
  • Flow is within what the top opening can pass

This covers most domestic and commercial softeners and filters.

When to choose side mount

  • Larger vessels, generally 1665 and above
  • Headroom is limited — a basement, or under a low ceiling
  • Higher flow than a top opening can carry
  • Frequent access to the vessel interior is expected

Headroom — measure before ordering

This catches people out. A top mount vessel needs clearance above it for the valve, and also for removing the valve later.

Add roughly 12 inches above the vessel for the valve, plus room to work. If that is not available, choose side mount.

The opening sizes

Top openings come in two common thread sizes:

  • 2.5 inch — smaller vessels, lower flow
  • 4 inch — larger vessels, higher flow

The valve must match. Where they differ, an adaptor 2.5 x 4 for top mounted MPV (₹210) is used.

How it affects valve selection

Multiport valves are made specifically for top mount or side mount — they are not interchangeable. So decide the mounting before ordering the valve.

Valve selection is covered in our MPV guide, and the code system in our MPV code guide.

The distribution system also differs

Top mount vessels use a riser pipe with a top distributor and bottom laterals. Options include the RM ST1101 top distribution (₹788) and lateral assemblies such as the RM ST6101 with 6 long laterals (₹2,520).

Larger vessels use hub and lateral arrangements such as the 6450 for a 2472 vessel (₹1,575) or the 6600 for a 3072 vessel (₹1,575). See our distribution guide.

Other fittings you will need

  • In/out adaptor with 20NB inlet and outlet — ₹578
  • Vacuum breaker / air release valve 25NB — ₹210, which prevents the vessel collapsing under vacuum when it drains
  • Brine director 15NB — ₹1,312, for softeners
The vacuum breaker is often left out and it should not be. If a vessel drains and air cannot get in, the vacuum can deform the vessel or pull media into the pipework. At ₹210 it is cheap protection.

  4. Application — softener or filter
  5. Whether the valve is manual or automatic

Frequently asked questions

What is the difference between top mount and side mount?

On a top mount vessel the valve screws into an opening on top, which is usual for smaller vessels and needs headroom above. On a side mount the valve bolts to a side opening, which suits larger vessels and works where headroom is limited.

How much headroom does a top mount vessel need?

Roughly twelve inches above the vessel for the valve, plus room to work. So a 1665 vessel of sixty five inches needs about six and a half feet of total height. If that is not available, choose side mount.

Can a top mount valve be used on a side mount vessel?

No, multiport valves are made specifically for one or the other and are not interchangeable. That is why the mounting arrangement has to be decided before ordering the valve.

What is the difference between 2.5 inch and 4 inch openings?

They are the thread size of the top opening. Smaller vessels with lower flow use two and a half inch, and larger vessels four inch. Where the valve and vessel differ, an adaptor is available.

Why is a vacuum breaker needed?

If a vessel drains and air cannot enter, the resulting vacuum can deform the vessel or draw media into the pipework. A vacuum breaker or air release valve prevents this and costs very little.
