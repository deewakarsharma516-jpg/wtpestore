Automatic RO permeate diversion using a conductivity controller

Aqua Filtration System, Faridabad · 9+ years in water treatment · Updated September 2026

Short answer: Using the relay on a conductivity controller you can make an RO plant look after itself — if permeate TDS rises, the plant stops or the water goes to drain, so poor quality water never reaches the tank. You need a CT 650 controller (₹5,880) and a solenoid valve.

Alternatively, take a ROMATIC controller (₹17,850), which has all of this built in.

The problem this solves

An RO membrane weakens gradually. One day permeate TDS starts rising — but the plant carries on running and the poor quality water goes straight into the tank.

You only find out when the whole tank is spoiled, or a customer complains. On larger plants this is a serious issue, particularly in packaged water and pharma.

The solution — automatic diversion

The controller continuously monitors permeate TDS. If it exceeds the set limit, the relay operates, and that relay can either:

  • Stop the plant — the simplest option
  • Send permeate to drain — the plant keeps running and the membrane keeps flushing (better)
  • Sound an alarm — so somebody comes to look
Why diversion is better: stopping the plant leaves water standing in the membrane, where bacteria can develop. With diversion the plant keeps running, the membrane keeps flushing, and as soon as TDS returns to normal the water goes back to the tank.

What setpoint to use

Set it somewhat above your normal reading — not too close, or the plant will keep diverting unnecessarily.

ApplicationNormal permeateSetpoint
Drinking water20–50 ppm80–100 ppm
Packaged water10–30 ppm50 ppm
Boiler feed5–20 ppm30 ppm
Industrial processAs required30% above requirement
Always set a dead band: for example divert at 100 and return to normal at 80. Without it the valve will keep opening and closing around the setpoint and will fail early.

Where to fit the sensor

  • On the permeate line, after the membrane and before the diverter valve
  • Where flow is continuous
  • Permanently immersed
  • Easy to remove for cleaning

A meter on the feed line as well is better still, since that gives you rejection. The formula is in our TDS guide.

Startup flush — another useful benefit

For the first few minutes after an RO plant starts, permeate TDS is high, because standing water is being flushed out of the membrane. Automatic control handles this too:

  1. Plant starts — TDS high, valve to drain
  2. Within 2 to 3 minutes TDS falls
  3. The valve switches automatically to the tank

So only good water ever enters the tank, without anyone having to think about it.

Wiring points

  1. Check the relay rating — a small solenoid valve can be connected directly, but a larger load needs a contactor
  2. Keep the sensor cable away from power cables — otherwise the reading becomes unstable
  3. Normally open or normally closed — choose the valve so that on power failure the water goes to drain, not to the tank. This is a safety point
This small detail matters: the default valve position on power failure should be drain. Otherwise, during a power cut, unchecked water goes straight into the tank.

Where this setup is essential

  • Packaged drinking water — quality cannot be compromised
  • Pharma and food — validation and audit
  • Large plants with big tanks — one bad batch spoils the whole tank
  • Unmanned plants — nobody is watching

Where it is not needed

  • Small plants where TDS is checked daily
  • Where the end use is not sensitive to a modest rise in TDS
  • Domestic RO systems

There a simple TDS meter (₹2,730) is sufficient — provided somebody makes a habit of looking at it.

Frequently asked questions

How does automatic TDS control work on an RO plant?

A conductivity controller continuously monitors permeate TDS. When it exceeds the set limit, its relay operates a solenoid valve which sends the water to drain, so that poor quality water does not reach the storage tank.

Is it better to stop the plant or divert the water?

Diverting is better. Stopping the plant leaves water standing inside the membrane where bacteria can develop, whereas with diversion the plant keeps running and the water returns to the tank as soon as TDS is normal again.

What setpoint should be used?

Around thirty to forty percent above your normal permeate reading. Setting it too close means the plant will keep diverting unnecessarily. Always set a dead band as well so the valve does not keep cycling.

What position should the valve default to on power failure?

To drain. That ensures unchecked water does not enter the tank during a power cut. It is a small detail but an important safety consideration when selecting the valve.

Should I buy a ROMATIC or separate components?

The ROMATIC brings everything into one unit with no wiring complications, but costs more. Separate components are cheaper although the wiring has to be done correctly. For large or critical plants the ROMATIC is generally better.
