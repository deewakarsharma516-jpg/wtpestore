Cartridge housing leaking — O-ring and thread problems

Aqua Filtration System, Faridabad · 9+ years in water treatment · Updated September 2026

Short answer: Around 90 percent of housing leaks are caused by a worn or badly seated O-ring. The fix is simple — remove it, clean it, apply silicone grease and seat it properly. If it is worn, replace it; O-rings are inexpensive.

The most common mistake: seeing a leak and tightening the housing harder. That wears the thread or cracks the housing, turning a small problem into a large one.

First, locate the leak

Where it leaksLikely cause
At the joint (middle of the housing)O-ring — most common
At the inlet or outlet threadInsufficient PTFE tape, or worn thread
At the air release valveValve loose or washer worn
From the housing bodyA crack — replacement needed
Around the pressure gaugeThread seal

Cause 1: The O-ring

What happens

  • Over time the rubber hardens and loses its elasticity
  • Repeated opening without grease wears it
  • It shifts out of position while changing a cartridge
  • It swells from chemical exposure or hot water

The fix

  1. Stop the pump and release pressure
  2. Open the housing and remove the O-ring
  3. Inspect it — replace it if flattened, cut or hardened
  4. Clean the groove it sits in — no grit or debris
  5. Apply a thin film of silicone grease
  6. Seat the O-ring properly in the groove, with no part protruding
  7. Close the housing — hand tight only
Why silicone grease specifically: ordinary oil or grease swells and degrades rubber. Silicone grease is safe for rubber and considerably extends O-ring life. It is inexpensive and one tube lasts years.

Cause 2: Over-tightening

People assume tightening harder will stop a leak, but the opposite happens:

  • The O-ring is flattened and stops sealing
  • The plastic thread wears
  • The housing can crack

The correct approach: tighten by hand until firm, then just a little more. Use a spanner for opening only, never for closing.

Cause 3: Thread problems

If the leak is at the inlet or outlet thread:

  1. Remove the fitting and strip off all the old PTFE tape
  2. Apply fresh tape in the direction of tightening
  3. Wrap four to six turns, no more
  4. Retighten — again without excessive force

If the thread itself is worn: use a brass insert housing (₹682–761), where brass is moulded into the thread so it does not wear.

Cause 4: The housing has aged

PP housings become brittle with time and sun exposure. Signs:

  • Colour change (yellowing or whitening)
  • Fine cracks appearing
  • A chalky feel to the surface

Replace such a housing immediately — it can fail at any moment. A new PP housing starts at ₹236; where it is exposed to sun, consider SS or relocating it.

Six ways to prevent recurrence

  1. Replace the O-ring every second or third cartridge change — it is cheap, leaks are not
  2. Always use silicone grease
  3. Tighten by hand, not with a spanner
  4. Keep the groove clean — a single grain of grit can cause a leak
  5. Protect the housing from sunlight, or use SS
  6. Keep spare O-rings so you can replace one immediately

When the housing must be replaced

  • A crack in the body
  • Thread completely worn (still leaking with fresh PTFE tape)
  • The plastic has become brittle
  • The O-ring groove itself is damaged

A safety note

Never open a housing under pressure. It is dangerous — the lid can be thrown off. Always:
  • Stop the pump
  • Close the line valve
  • Release pressure through the air release valve
  • Only then open it
This is precisely why a housing with an air release valve is worth having, for only ₹26 more.

The hidden cost of a small leak

People often ignore a small drip, but:

  • Water reaching panels and wiring — a short circuit risk
  • Algae on the floor — a slipping hazard
  • Reduced pressure — plant flow falls
  • Wasted water

So fix a leak as soon as you see it — it is a ten minute job.

Frequently asked questions

Why is my cartridge housing leaking?

In around ninety percent of cases the cause is a worn or badly seated O-ring. It can also leak at the thread where PTFE tape is insufficient, or because the housing has aged and become brittle.

Should I tighten the housing harder if it leaks?

No. Over-tightening flattens the O-ring so it stops sealing, and it wears the plastic thread or cracks the housing. Hand tightening is correct.

What grease should be used on an O-ring?

Only silicone grease. Ordinary oil or grease swells and degrades rubber, whereas silicone grease is safe for rubber and considerably extends O-ring life.

How often should the O-ring be replaced?

Every second or third cartridge change. O-rings are inexpensive while a leak can cause real damage, so this is not worth economising on.

What should I do before opening a housing?

Stop the pump, close the line valve and release all pressure through the air release valve. Opening a housing under pressure is dangerous because the lid can be thrown off.
