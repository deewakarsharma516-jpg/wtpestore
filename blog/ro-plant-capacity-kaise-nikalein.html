How to size an RO plant — capacity, recovery and feed flow

Aqua Filtration System, Faridabad · 9+ years in water treatment · Updated September 2026

Short answer: Start from your daily requirement, decide how many hours a day the plant will run, and that gives the LPH rating. Then work backwards through recovery to find the feed flow, which sizes the pump, pre-treatment and pipework.

Plant LPH = daily requirement ÷ running hours
Feed LPH = plant LPH ÷ recovery

Step 1: Daily requirement

Be honest about this, and include peak days rather than the average:

ApplicationTypical demand
Small office drinking water200 – 500 L/day
Restaurant500 – 2,000 L/day
Hotel, 30 rooms3,000 – 6,000 L/day
Packaged water unitAs per production plan
Boiler feedSteam output plus blowdown
Process waterPer process requirement

Step 2: Running hours

Fewer hours means a bigger plant. There is a genuine trade-off:

Running hoursFor 5,000 L/dayComment
4 hours1,250 LPHLarger plant, higher cost
8 hours625 LPHUsual choice
16 hours313 LPHSmaller plant, needs storage
24 hours208 LPHSmallest plant, no downtime allowance
Do not size on 24 hours. The plant needs time for CIP, cartridge changes and breakdowns. Size on 16 to 18 hours maximum, so there is margin.

Step 3: Choose the recovery

Recovery is what proportion of feed water becomes permeate. It is limited by feed TDS, because the reject stream concentrates as recovery rises.

Feed TDSSafe recoveryReject per 1,000 L permeate
Below 50070 – 75%~350 L
500 – 1,00065 – 70%~470 L
1,000 – 2,00060 – 65%~600 L
2,000 – 4,00050 – 60%~800 L
Above 4,00040 – 50%~1,200 L

Pushing recovery higher scales membranes — see our membrane life guide.

Worked example

Requirement: hotel needing 5,000 L/day, feed TDS 1,200 ppm, running 8 hours.

  1. Plant capacity: 5,000 ÷ 8 = 625 LPH, so specify 750 LPH with margin
  2. Recovery at 1,200 TDS: 65 percent
  3. Feed flow: 750 ÷ 0.65 = 1,154 LPH
  4. Reject flow: 1,154 − 750 = 404 LPH
  5. Daily feed water needed: 1,154 × 8 = 9,232 litres
That last figure matters. To make 5,000 litres you need over 9,000 litres of raw water. If your borewell cannot supply that, the plant will not run regardless of how it is specified.

Step 4: Size the pre-treatment

Everything upstream is sized on feed flow (1,154 LPH), not plant output:

  • Feed pump — 1,154 LPH at the required pressure
  • Multigrade or sand filter — if turbidity is present
  • Softener — if hardness is high, sized on feed flow. See our softener sizing guide
  • Carbon filter — if chlorine is present
  • Cartridge filter 5 micron — sized for 1,154 LPH. See our micron guide
  • Dosing systems — antiscalant and SMBS, dosed on feed flow

This is the most common sizing error we see — pre-treatment sized on plant output rather than feed flow, so it chokes as soon as the plant runs.

Step 5: Membranes

For 750 LPH at 1,200 TDS you would typically use 2 to 3 × 4040 elements. See our membrane sizing guide.

Step 6: Storage

Storage should cover the gap between production and consumption. As a guide:

  • Raw water tank: enough for the daily feed requirement, so the plant is not dependent on borewell timing
  • Treated water tank: at least half a day's requirement, so consumption can continue during CIP or maintenance

Instruments to include

These are not optional extras — without them you cannot tell what the plant is doing:

  • Rotameters on permeate and reject — see our placement guide
  • Pressure gauges before and after the cartridge and membrane
  • TDS meters on feed and permeate
  • ROMATIC controller for automatic operation and TDS diversion

Frequently asked questions

How do I calculate RO plant capacity?

Divide your daily requirement by the running hours. For five thousand litres a day over eight hours you need about six hundred and twenty five LPH, and you would specify seven hundred and fifty LPH to allow margin.

What recovery should an RO plant run at?

It depends on feed TDS. Seventy to seventy five percent below five hundred TDS, sixty to sixty five percent between one and two thousand, and fifty percent or less above four thousand. Pushing higher scales the membranes.

How much raw water does an RO plant need?

Divide plant output by recovery. At sixty five percent recovery, producing seven hundred and fifty litres per hour needs about eleven hundred and fifty litres of feed. Check your borewell can actually supply that.

Should pre-treatment be sized on plant output or feed flow?

Always on feed flow, which is higher than plant output. Sizing the cartridge filter, softener and pumps on plant output is the most common design error and causes the pre-treatment to choke immediately.

Can I size the plant for twenty four hour running?

No, because the plant needs time for CIP cleaning, cartridge changes and breakdowns. Size on sixteen to eighteen hours maximum so there is margin for maintenance and unexpected stops.
