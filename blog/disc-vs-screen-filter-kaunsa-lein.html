Disc filter vs screen filter — which one to choose

Aqua Filtration System, Faridabad · 9+ years in water treatment · Updated September 2026

Short answer: A screen filter uses a mesh that retains particles on its surface — inexpensive and easy to clean. A disc filter uses stacked grooved discs that filter in depth, so it holds more dirt, at slightly higher cost.

Both are washed and reused, so fitting one ahead of your cartridges substantially reduces cartridge cost.

Y type or T type

  • Y type: the filter element sits at an angle, forming a Y shape. Common and economical in smaller sizes
  • T type: the element sits vertically below. Better in larger sizes, easier to clean and with more capacity

Y type is fine up to 1.5 inch; from 2 inch upward T type is preferable.

The real benefit of fitting one

These filters go ahead of the cartridges. The benefits:

  • Coarse particles are stopped here
  • The load on cartridges falls sharply — they last two to three times longer
  • They are washed and reused, so there is no recurring cost
  • A one-off purchase that serves for years
A real calculation: if a ₹457 Y-type screen filter means you use one cartridge a month instead of two, you save around ₹105 a month — so it pays for itself in four to five months, and saves money for years afterwards.

How often to clean

  • Clean water: once a month
  • Moderate: once a week
  • Very dirty: every two to three days

The reliable method: watch the pressure difference across the filter. When it exceeds 0.5 kg/cm², clean it.

Cleaning method

Screen filter

  1. Isolate the line and open the drain plug
  2. Remove the mesh element
  3. Flush with water from the reverse side (backwash)
  4. Brush gently
  5. Refit, checking the O-ring

Disc filter

  1. Isolate the line and open the housing
  2. Remove the disc stack — note the order
  3. Separate the discs and rinse them (rubbing gently)
  4. Reassemble in the same order, compressed firmly
The most common error with disc filters: not compressing the discs firmly on reassembly. Water then passes between loose discs unfiltered — and there is no outward sign that anything is wrong.

Automatic self-cleaning versions

For very dirty water or large flows there are automatic filters that backwash themselves. They cost more, but where cleaning would otherwise be a daily task they are worth considering — please contact us separately about these.

The full pre-treatment sequence

  1. Screen or disc filter — coarse particles, washed and reused
  2. Sand or multigrade filter — turbidity, if it is high
  3. Bag filter — medium particles, if required
  4. Cartridge 5 micron — final protection
  5. RO membrane

Not every plant needs all these stages — select according to your water. The details are in our micron guide.

Frequently asked questions

What is the difference between a disc filter and a screen filter?

A screen filter uses a mesh that retains particles on its surface, while a disc filter uses stacked grooved discs that filter in depth. The disc type therefore holds more dirt but costs a little more.

Why should these filters go ahead of the cartridges?

Because they stop the coarse particles, which reduces the load on the cartridges so they last two to three times longer. And since they are washed and reused there is no recurring cost.

What is the difference between Y type and T type?

In a Y type the element sits at an angle, which is common and economical in smaller sizes. In a T type it sits vertically below, which is better in larger sizes because cleaning is easier and capacity is greater.

How often should these filters be cleaned?

Once a month on clean water, weekly on moderately dirty water, and every two to three days on very dirty water. The reliable method is to watch the pressure difference and clean when it exceeds half a kg per cm2.

What should I watch when cleaning a disc filter?

Reassemble the discs in the same order and compress them firmly. Loose discs allow water to pass between them unfiltered, and there is no outward sign that this is happening, so the fault goes unnoticed.
