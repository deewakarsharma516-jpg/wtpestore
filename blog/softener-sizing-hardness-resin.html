Water softener sizing — calculating resin from hardness

Aqua Filtration System, Faridabad · 9+ years in water treatment · Updated September 2026

Short answer: Softener sizing comes down to one calculation. You need the hardness of your water in ppm, your daily consumption in litres, and how many days between regenerations you want. From those three the resin volume follows, and the resin volume decides the vessel.

Resin (litres) = (hardness ppm × litres per day × days between regeneration) ÷ 55,000

Step 1: Measure the hardness

This is the figure everything depends on, so do not guess it. Use a hardness test kit (4 bottle, 100 ml) at ₹1,050, or the 30 ml version at ₹656.

A flow meter on the line gives you the real figure, which is always better than an estimate.

Step 3: Decide the regeneration interval

How often are you willing to regenerate?

  • Every 2 to 3 days: smaller vessel, less initial cost, more effort and more salt over time
  • Every 7 to 15 days: the usual compromise
  • Every 20 to 30 days: large vessel, high initial cost, but the resin sits exhausted for long periods

Most installations settle on 7 to 15 days.

Worked example

A small hotel: hardness 350 ppm, consumption 5,000 litres a day, regenerating every 10 days.

Resin = (350 × 5,000 × 10) ÷ 55,000 = 318 litres

Looking at the vessel chart, 318 litres needs a 3072 vessel (500 L capacity) — a 2469 holds only 307 L, which is just short.

Alternatively, regenerate every 8 days instead of 10: resin = 255 litres, which fits comfortably in a 2469 vessel and costs considerably less. This is the kind of trade-off worth working through before ordering.

Also check the flow

Resin volume is one constraint; service flow is the other. Water passing too quickly through the bed is not fully softened.

The usual limit is around 20 to 25 litres per minute per square foot of bed area. So:

  • If your peak flow is high but hardness is low, size on flow
  • If hardness is high but flow is low, size on resin
  • Take whichever gives the larger vessel

The complete system

A softener is not just a vessel. You will also need:

  • FRP vessel of the calculated size
  • Softener multiport valve — see our MPV selection guide. Note that a filter valve will not work
  • Distribution system — top distributor and laterals
  • Cation resin of the calculated volume, plus graded gravel
  • Brine tank with a brine director
  • Vacuum breaker — ₹210

Salt consumption

Roughly 100 to 150 grams of salt per litre of resin per regeneration. For the example above with 255 litres of resin, that is around 30 kg per regeneration, and regenerating every 8 days works out to roughly 115 kg of salt a month.

This is worth calculating before ordering, because salt is a recurring cost.

Common sizing mistakes

  1. Guessing the hardness — a test kit costs ₹656 and removes all doubt
  2. Using average flow instead of peak flow — the softener has to cope with peak demand
  3. Choosing too long an interval — a very large vessel costs more and the resin sits exhausted
  4. Ignoring the flow limit — a vessel correctly sized on resin may still be too small on flow
  5. Forgetting the salt cost — a large softener regenerating frequently uses a lot of salt

Frequently asked questions

How do I calculate the resin volume for a softener?

Multiply the hardness in ppm by the litres used per day and by the days between regenerations, then divide by fifty five thousand. For example three hundred and fifty ppm at five thousand litres a day over ten days needs three hundred and eighteen litres of resin.

How do I find out my water hardness?

Use a hardness test kit, which costs from about six hundred and fifty rupees. Guessing is the most common sizing mistake, because the whole calculation depends on this figure.

What regeneration interval should I choose?

Most installations settle on seven to fifteen days. A shorter interval means a smaller vessel but more effort and more salt over time, while a very long interval means a large expensive vessel with the resin sitting exhausted.

Should I size the softener on flow or on resin volume?

Check both and take whichever gives the larger vessel. If peak flow is high but hardness low, flow decides it. If hardness is high but flow low, the resin volume decides it.

How much salt will a softener use?

Roughly one hundred to one hundred and fifty grams per litre of resin per regeneration. It is worth calculating the monthly figure before ordering, because salt is a recurring cost.
