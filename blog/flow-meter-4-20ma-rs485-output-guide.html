4-20mA and RS485 outputs explained — when you need them

Aqua Filtration System, Faridabad · 9+ years in water treatment · Updated September 2026

Short answer: Both are ways of sending meter data somewhere else. 4-20mA is the older, very reliable method that carries a single value such as flow, and suits a panel or PLC. RS485 is digital, can carry several meters on one pair of wires and sends more data, which suits a computer, datalogger or remote monitoring.

If you only ever read the meter standing next to it, you need neither — and you save the money.

What 4-20mA actually is

The meter sends its reading as a current signal. 4 milliamps means minimum and 20 milliamps means maximum, with everything in between proportional.

So on a meter ranged 0 to 1,000 LPH: 4mA = 0 LPH, 12mA = 500 LPH, 20mA = 1,000 LPH.

Its strength: a current signal does not weaken over long distances and tolerates a certain amount of electrical noise. That is why it has been the industrial standard for decades.

Its limitation: one pair of wires carries one value only. If you need both flow and total, you need two connections.

What RS485 is

This is digital communication. The meter sends its complete data — flow, total, status — as numbers. The big advantage is that several meters can share one pair of wires, typically ten to thirty of them.

Imagine six meters around a plant. With 4-20mA each needs its own cable run to the control room — six cables. With RS485 they all connect along a single loop.

Where the data goes — four options

1. Just a larger display

The meter is in an awkward position and the reading needs to be visible from a distance. Use a Universal Indicator UT-650 (₹7,035), or the version with output, UT-650 with 4-20mA/RS485 (₹10,500).

2. Keeping records for audit or validation

A DL-650 datalogger with 8GB card (₹11,550) writes every reading to a memory card automatically. This is often mandatory in pharma and food plants.

3. Viewing on a computer

An RS485 to USB converter (₹6,300) connects the meter to a laptop.

4. On a mobile or server, from anywhere

A 4G modem with RS485 input (₹9,450) sends data over the internet, which suits pollution board monitoring or multi-site plants. A telemetry modem (₹13,650) does the same job.

When you do not need any of this

  • A small RO plant where the operator reads the meter daily
  • Domestic or small commercial installations
  • Anywhere with no record-keeping or audit requirement

In those cases a ₹504 rotameter is the sensible choice. An output add-on only pays for itself when the data is genuinely used.

A common misconception

People often assume that adding a 4-20mA output makes the reading more accurate. It does not. An output does not change accuracy — the meter measures exactly as well as it did before. The output only carries that reading somewhere else.

Frequently asked questions

Which is better, 4-20mA or RS485?

They do different jobs. If you only need to send one value to a panel or PLC, 4-20mA is simple and very reliable. If you need full data from several meters carried to a computer or datalogger on a single cable, RS485 is better.

Can an output be added later?

Usually not. It is part of the instrument itself, so it has to be ordered at the time of purchase. Adding it afterwards normally means replacing the whole unit, which is considerably more expensive.

Does adding an output improve accuracy?

No. The output has nothing to do with accuracy. The meter measures exactly as it did before, and the output simply carries that reading to a panel, computer or server.

How many meters can share one RS485 cable?

Typically ten to thirty devices, and the cable can run over a kilometre. Each device is given its own address so the data does not get mixed up.

What is needed for pollution board monitoring?

An instrument with an output, along with a telemetry or 4G modem that sends the data to the board server. This usually requires an electromagnetic flow meter and the associated sensors, and we can supply the complete list.
