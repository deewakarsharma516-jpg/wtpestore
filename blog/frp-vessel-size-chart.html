FRP vessel size chart — 0844 to 3072 with capacities

Aqua Filtration System, Faridabad · 9+ years in water treatment · Updated September 2026

Short answer: The four digit vessel code is simply the diameter and height in inches. A 1054 is 10 inches in diameter and 54 inches tall; a 1665 is 16 by 65 inches. Vessel size is selected from the volume of media you need to hold, with the media occupying no more than about 60 percent of the vessel.

Reading the code

Every FRP vessel carries a four digit number:

  • The first two digits are the diameter in inches
  • The last two digits are the height in inches

So a 1354 is 13 inches across and 54 inches tall, and a 2472 is 24 by 72 inches.

How to use this chart: work out the resin volume you need first, then pick the smallest vessel that will hold it. For 96 litres of resin choose a 1465, not a 1665 — the larger vessel simply costs more without any benefit.

Working out the resin volume for a softener

Two figures decide it — the hardness of your water and how much water you treat between regenerations.

Resin (litres) = (hardness in ppm × litres per day × days between regeneration) ÷ 55,000

Example: 350 ppm hardness, 1,000 litres a day, regenerating every 15 days:
(350 × 1,000 × 15) ÷ 55,000 = 95 litres of resin → so a 1465 vessel.

Why media should not exceed 60 percent

Media must never fill the vessel. Space is needed above it — called freeboard — because during backwash the media bed expands and lifts.

  • Without freeboard the media has nowhere to expand, so backwash does not clean it
  • Media can be carried out through the top distributor and lost
  • The bed becomes compacted and channels form

Choosing by flow, not only by media

Media volume is one constraint; flow is the other. Water passing too fast through the bed is not properly treated.

The usual guide is a service flow of 20 to 25 litres per minute per square foot of bed area. In practice this means:

  • If your flow is high but hardness is low, choose the vessel on flow
  • If hardness is high but flow is low, choose it on resin volume
  • Take whichever comes out larger

Frequently asked questions

What does the FRP vessel number mean?

It is the diameter and height in inches. A 1054 vessel is ten inches in diameter and fifty four inches tall, and a 1665 is sixteen by sixty five inches.

How do I work out the resin volume for a softener?

Multiply the hardness in ppm by the litres treated per day and by the days between regenerations, then divide by fifty five thousand. For example three hundred and fifty ppm at one thousand litres a day over fifteen days gives ninety five litres of resin.

Why should media not fill more than sixty percent of the vessel?

Because the bed expands and lifts during backwash and needs space above it, known as freeboard. Without that space the backwash cannot clean the media, and media can be carried out through the top distributor.

Should I choose the vessel by flow or by media volume?

Check both and take whichever gives the larger vessel. If flow is high but hardness low, flow decides it. If hardness is high but flow low, the resin volume decides it.

What else is needed besides the vessel?

A multiport valve, a distribution system with top distributor and laterals, the media itself, and for a softener a brine tank with a brine director. A vessel alone is not a working system.
