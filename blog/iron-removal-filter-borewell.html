Iron removal filter for borewell water — fixing yellow water and stains

Aqua Filtration System, Faridabad · 9+ years in water treatment · Updated October 2026

Short answer: Borewell water often carries dissolved iron. It comes out clear, then turns yellow or brown on standing and leaves rust stains on tiles, buckets and clothes. The fix is to oxidise the iron (aeration or chlorine dosing) so it becomes particles, then filter it out in a multigrade or iron-removal filter, followed by carbon to remove excess chlorine.

Signs of iron in water

  • Water is clear at the tap but turns yellow in a bucket after some time
  • Orange-brown stains on tiles, wash basins and toilets
  • Metallic taste; tea turns dark
  • White clothes turn yellowish
  • RO membranes and softener resin foul quickly

Test before you buy

Get the iron (Fe) value in ppm tested, along with pH, TDS and hardness. The drinking water limit is 0.3 ppm; above 1 ppm the staining becomes obvious. See what to test.

How iron is removed

Iron levelTypical setup
Up to ~1 ppmAeration or light chlorine dosing → multigrade filter → carbon filter
1 – 5 ppmChlorine / oxidant dosing with contact time → MGF or iron-removal media → carbon filter
Above 5 ppmAeration tank + settling, then filtration — needs a designed system

The oxidation step is what most people miss. A sand filter alone cannot catch dissolved iron — it passes straight through and turns yellow later in the tank.

The equipment

  • Dosing pump for chlorine — see how to select one
  • Multigrade filter to trap the oxidised iron — backwash daily, because iron loads the bed quickly
  • Activated carbon filter to remove leftover chlorine and smell
  • Softener or RO after this, if hardness or TDS is also high

Why iron must be removed before a softener or RO

Iron coats softener resin and cannot be fully washed out by salt regeneration. In an RO, oxidised iron fouls the membrane surface. Removing iron first protects both and avoids frequent CIP cleaning.

Frequently asked questions

Why does borewell water turn yellow after standing?

Because dissolved iron reacts with air and becomes rust particles, which give the yellow or brown colour and stain surfaces.

What is the safe limit of iron in drinking water?

The drinking water limit is 0.3 ppm. Above about 1 ppm, staining and taste become obvious.

Can a sand filter remove iron?

Only after the iron is oxidised by aeration or chlorine. Dissolved iron passes through a sand filter.

Will a water softener remove iron?

A softener can remove small amounts but the iron fouls the resin. It is better to remove iron before the softener.

How often should an iron removal filter be backwashed?

Daily, because oxidised iron loads the filter bed quickly.
