Organic fouling and biofilm

Organic fouling behaves differently from scale. Instead of hard crystals, it forms a soft, slippery film across the membrane — bacteria, extracellular slime, oils, humic matter from surface water and fine colloidal clay all bind together into a layer that water has to push through.

The tell-tale signature is a sharp rise in differential pressure across the first stage while salt rejection stays reasonably good. Scale usually hits the last stage hardest because that is where salts concentrate; biofilm hits the first stage, where nutrients enter.

Biofilm is also self-renewing. Any bacteria left behind after a partial clean multiply again within days, which is why organic fouling seems to come back faster each time if it is not fully removed.

How ZS-RO-103 works

CIP-B raises the pH of the cleaning solution, which breaks down organic material and lifts it off the membrane:

  • Saponifies oils and fats — converts greasy deposits into water-soluble forms that rinse away with the cleaning flow.
  • Breaks up biofilm structure — high pH destabilises the slime matrix holding bacterial colonies to the surface.
  • Disperses colloids — keeps fine clay and silt particles suspended so they leave with the flush instead of resettling.

For plants that foul both ways — mineral and organic — run the acidic clean first, flush thoroughly, then run this alkaline stage.

Common questions

How do I know whether I need acidic or alkaline cleaner?

Look at where the pressure rises. Rising differential pressure in the first stage with rejection holding usually means organic fouling — use alkaline. Falling flow with rising salt passage in the last stage points to scale — use acidic. When in doubt, or when both symptoms appear, run acid first then alkaline.

Can I mix acidic and alkaline cleaners together?

Never. They neutralise each other and can react. Run them as separate stages with a thorough permeate flush in between.

How long should the alkaline soak be?

1 hour for light fouling. Established biofilm needs longer — many plants soak overnight and then run the high-flow stage in the morning.

Why does biofilm keep coming back?

Because bacteria left behind multiply. Repeat fouling within weeks means the source is upstream: check for stagnant storage, exhausted carbon media or dead-legs in piping, and consider a periodic biocide dose.

Is it safe for all membranes?

It suits standard thin-film composite membranes at recommended strength and temperature. Confirm your membrane's maximum pH and temperature before cleaning — send us the model if you are not sure.

Can this be used for UF membranes too?
