What scaling does to an RO plant

Every borewell or municipal feed water carries dissolved calcium, magnesium, barium and silica. Inside an RO membrane roughly 60–75% of that water passes through as permeate — but the salts do not. They stay behind in the shrinking reject stream, and their concentration climbs until they cross their solubility limit and start forming crystals directly on the membrane surface.

Once that layer forms, three things follow in order: permeate flow drops, reject pressure climbs, and salt rejection falls so your product TDS rises. Operators usually notice the TDS first — by then the scale has been growing for a while. Hard scale that has bonded to the membrane often cannot be fully cleaned off, so the membrane is permanently down on output.

Antiscalant is what stops that sequence from starting.

How ZS-RO-101 works

ZS-RO-101 is a blend of dispersant polymers and threshold inhibitors. It does not remove hardness from the water — it interferes with crystal formation at a chemical level:

  • Threshold inhibition — allows scale-forming salts to stay dissolved past their normal saturation point, so they never precipitate.
  • Crystal distortion — any crystals that do begin forming grow into irregular shapes that cannot pack into a hard adherent layer.
  • Dispersion — keeps fine particles suspended in the reject stream so they flush to drain instead of settling on the membrane.

The practical result is a longer run between CIP cleanings, steadier permeate output, and membranes that reach their rated life instead of being replaced early.

Common questions

How much antiscalant should I dose?

2 to 5 ppm on feed water. Low-TDS feed sits at the 2–3 ppm end; high hardness, high silica or high recovery pushes you to 4–5 ppm. Share your water report and we will confirm the figure for your plant.

Can I dose it neat, or should I dilute it?

Both work. Neat dosing is fine, but most plants dilute in the dosing tank so the pump runs at a workable stroke setting. Always dilute with RO permeate or soft water — raw hard water in the dosing tank defeats the purpose.

Where exactly does it go into the plant?

After the cartridge filter and before the high pressure pump. That gives the chemical time to mix fully with the feed before it reaches the membrane.

What happens if dosing stops for a few days?

Scale begins forming within days on hard feed water. You will see permeate flow drop and reject pressure rise first. Restart dosing immediately and plan a CIP — a scaled membrane left untreated does not fully recover.

Is it safe for drinking water plants?

Yes, at recommended dose levels. The antiscalant leaves with the reject stream and is not carried into permeate.

Do I still need CIP chemicals if I dose antiscalant?

Yes — antiscalant stretches the interval between cleanings, it does not replace them. Keep acidic and alkaline CIP chemicals on hand for scheduled cleaning.
